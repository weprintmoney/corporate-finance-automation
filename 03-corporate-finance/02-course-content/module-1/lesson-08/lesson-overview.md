# Lesson 8 — Regression Betas

## Overview

It is standard practice in finance to estimate a beta for a company by running a regression of its return on the market, and using the resulting slope as the beta. While the beta regression provides other useful information about the company, it is backward looking and noisy. This lesson breaks down a regression of stock returns against the market and reads the output: the intercept becomes a proxy for how good or bad your stock was as an investment during the regression period, the slope a measure of beta, and the R-squared the percentage of the company's risk that comes from the market.

**Learning objectives:**
- Run and interpret a regression of stock returns against market returns
- Use the regression intercept as an investment performance measure (Jensen's alpha)
- Interpret beta (slope) and R-squared from a regression
- Understand the limitations of regression betas: noise, estimation period sensitivity, and backward-looking bias
