Coca-Cola is a good subject because every corporate finance choice is visible in audited filings: a 64-year dividend record, a large debt book in two currencies, a string of bottler sales, and a disclosed $14 billion tax contingency (`facts.irs-potential-liability-2010-2025`).
Life cycle: mature. Reported revenue grew from $45,754M in 2023 to $47,941M in 2025, 2.36% a year (`forecast.stress_cases[0]`); operating income before impairments rose from $13,052M to $14,814M (`model-output.md` section 8 by year). Cash goes to dividends first ($8,779M in 2025, `company.history`). The one young behaviour is buying brands: BodyArmor has been written down by $1,720M in two years (`facts.bodyarmor-impairments-2024-2025`) and fairlife cost a $6,173M final milestone payment (`facts.fairlife-final-milestone`). Otherwise the company acts its age: it is selling bottlers and stakes, not building them.
