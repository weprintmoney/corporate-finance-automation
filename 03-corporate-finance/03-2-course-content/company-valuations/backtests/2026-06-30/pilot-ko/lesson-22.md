## Cumulative Project — Questions for This Lesson

- **Is your firm under, over or correctly levered?** (`l22-q1`, signal: leverage)

Under-levered: debt is $39.7 billion below the cost-of-capital optimum (`model-output.md` section 3).

- **If under levered, is it under threat of a hostile acquisition? If over levered, how imminent is the bankruptcy threat?** (`l22-q2`, signal: leverage)

No realistic hostile threat. At $349.7 billion of equity (`company.equity_value`) a bidder would need about $400 billion; a 9.29% long-term holder (Berkshire) sits on the register; and the gain from re-levering is only $2.12 a share (section 3), too small to pay a premium. Pressure, if any, would come from an activist, and none appears in the filing index (`sources/filings/index.md` lists two Schedule 13D/A amendments in 2025 and no new Schedule 13D).

- **How good are the potential projects that the company can invest in?** (`l22-q3`, signal: returns)

Mixed. The existing franchise earns 24%–27% (section 8), but incremental opportunities are acquisitions priced to the seller's growth story (BodyArmor, fairlife) and digital and marketing spending whose returns are not disclosed. Capex guidance is only $2.2 billion for 2026 (`facts.capex-guidance-2026`), about two times D&A of $1,050M (`company.da`). Good projects exist but are small; the large uses of cash have been purchases at full prices.

- **What is the best path that you see for your firm to get from its actual to its optimal debt ratio?** (`l22-q4`, signal: leverage)

The course's answer for an under-levered firm with few good projects is debt-funded buybacks or a special dividend, moving gradually. For Coca-Cola the binding constraint is the tax case: up to $14 billion of further liability (`facts.irs-potential-liability-2010-2025`) if the Eleventh Circuit upholds the Tax Court, and the 10-K says borrowing capacity is how it would pay (10-K line 1328). The sensible path is to wait for the appeal, then (if it wins) recover the $6.0 billion deposit and buy back stock with it plus new debt over two to three years; if it loses, borrowing to pay the liability moves it toward the optimum anyway.
