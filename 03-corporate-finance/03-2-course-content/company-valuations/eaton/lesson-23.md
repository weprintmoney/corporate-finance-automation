## Cumulative Project — Questions for This Lesson

- **Given its asset/project characteristics, what is the right type of debt for your firm?**
  Long-term, mostly fixed-rate debt in dollars and euros, in proportion to cash flows. The assets are plants and acquired franchises with lives over ten years, about 62% of sales are in the US, 18.5% in Europe (`country.exposures`), and pricing tends to keep up with inflation over time.

- **What are the actual debt characteristics?**
  Mixed rate: fixed-rate notes plus $2,497M of floating commercial paper, about 12% of borrowings (`debt.rate_type`, `facts.commercial-paper-2026-03-31`). Weighted maturity is about 7.5 years (`debt.maturity_years`, estimate, range 6.5-8.5). About 22% is in euros (`debt.currency`), partly designated as net-investment hedges. The notes carry no maintenance leverage covenant (`debt.covenants`). The March 2026 notes run from 2028 to 2056.

- **If there is a mismatch, what is the best way to fix it?**
  Little mismatch. Euro debt slightly exceeds the euro share of sales, which is fine as a hedge of European assets. The one short-term piece is the commercial paper, which the company appears to intend to repay from cash flow and the Mobility distribution (sources/statements/etn-stated-positions.md S5). The design fits the business; it was also clearly sized for the Boyd deal (S6).

## Notes

The debt fits the assets. No debt-design gap worth a goal.
