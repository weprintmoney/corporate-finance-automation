# Eaton Corporation plc (ETN) — Lesson 22: Moving to the Optimal Debt Ratio

### Lesson 22 — Moving to the Optimal Debt Ratio

Quick mode: only the question-bank entries with a `signals` field are answered here. No transcript-layer entries carry a signal, so none are listed.

## Cumulative Project — Questions for This Lesson

- **Is your firm under, over or correctly levered?**
  Slightly under-levered against its own optimum (11.70% against 18%) and over-levered against its sector: the peer median debt ratio is 5.8% (`facts.peer-median-debt-ratio`). Six months earlier it was at 6.07% against an optimum of 20% (alternatives/debt-at-2025-year-end-output.md). The acquisitions closed most of the gap in one quarter.

- **If under levered, is it under threat of a hostile acquisition? If over levered, how imminent is the bankruptcy threat?**
  No hostile threat: a $165B market cap with no blockholder, and a value-of-moving gain of only $1.12-$4.89 a share (model-output.md section 6) offers a raider nothing. No bankruptcy threat: A-/A3 ratings, a $4,000M undrawn revolver, and only 706 of notes maturing in 2027 (`facts.debt-maturity-2027`).

- **How good are the potential projects that the company can invest in?**
  Mixed. Organic projects in Electrical are strong: backlog up 48% (`facts.electrical-backlog-growth-q1-2026`) and segment margins of 24.5% (`facts.segment-margin-2025`) on capacity that earns well above 9.69%. The acquired projects start at an estimated 4.6-4.8% pre-tax yield (lesson 14). The company is filling a pipeline of good organic projects and paying high prices for growth outside it.

- **What is the best path that you see for your firm to get from its actual to its optimal debt ratio?**
  It needs no new move. Its stated path (no buybacks in 2026, Mobility cash of about $1,100M to repay debt, `facts.mobility-cash-distribution`) takes it away from the optimum, toward the peer median. Since the gain from moving is small and the stress-case optimum is 13%, holding about 12% and funding growth from cash flow is defensible. If it wants the optimum, the path is to resume buybacks funded with long-term notes once Mobility closes, not new acquisitions.

## Notes

Eaton is near enough to its optimum that the leverage decision isn't worth much in value. What matters is where the debt went: into acquisitions rather than buybacks.
