# SolarWinds (SWI) — Lesson 04: Define and Measure Risk

### Lesson 04 — Define and Measure Risk

- **Lesson 04, Session 4 · Part 1 — "Danger Plus Opportunity — The Ingredients of a Good Risk and Return Model"** ([transcript](../../01-foundations-and-discount-rates/lesson-04/session-4-part-1.md))
    - Quote: "Risk is equal to danger plus opportunity."
    - Question for SWI: Testing CAPM's single-beta framework against the lesson's five requirements — does it adequately price SolarWinds' concentrated federal/government customer base and demonstrated tail risk (a nation-state supply-chain attack), or does that risk fall into the "diversifiable, unpriced" bucket even though it clearly hit the actual marginal shareholder, Silver Lake, who was not diversified?
    - Sources needed: SWI government/federal customer revenue concentration disclosure, confirmation of Silver Lake's ownership concentration and trading behavior.
    - Where to find: SEC-EDGAR — SWI 10-K FY2024, Item 1A (Risk Factors, customer concentration) and Item 1 (business/customer mix); REPO-SWI-VAL — existing marginal-investor analysis (Step 4) for Silver Lake's diversification status.
    - Answer: CAPM handled this correctly, and the question's framing is wrong on two counts. First, the concentration premise isn't supported: SolarWinds never quantifies federal revenue, saying only that a portion of self-hosted license and time-based subscription sales goes to "a number of different departments of the U.S. federal government" and that "in certain calendar quarters, particularly the first and third calendar quarters, this portion may be meaningful" (swi-10k-fy2024.md, Item 1A). With over 300,000 customers, 1,148 of them above $100K of Total ARR, and no single customer at 10% of revenue, this is diffuse, not concentrated — the real concentration was in one *product*: Orion generated ~$343M, about 45% of revenue, in the first nine months of 2020 (swi-8k-2020-12-14-sunburst-followup.md). Second, Silver Lake alone held 36.56%, not ~75% — Thoma Bravo held a further 29.79% (swi-def14a-2024.md, beneficial ownership as of 3/28/2024) — and the sponsors together cut their stake from 88.8% to 65.0% between 2018 and 2024, which is diversifying behavior, not a locked concentrated bet. The deeper point is that SUNBURST-type risk was priced, just not through beta: it showed up as a permanent ~3–4 turn haircut to the EBITDA multiple, i.e., a level effect on price and expected cash flows. Asking a single-beta model to carry catastrophic firm-specific risk is a category error; put it in the cash flows and the price, and leave beta to measure covariance.

- **Lesson 04, Session 4 · Part 2 — "Climbing the Risk Ladder — From Firm-Specific Risk to the Market Portfolio"** ([transcript](../../01-foundations-and-discount-rates/lesson-04/session-4-part-2.md))
    - Quote: "Beta is not a measure of total risk."
    - Question for SWI: The lesson's "climbing the ladder" logic treats a firm-specific shock (a bad movie for Disney) as diversifiable — SUNBURST looks like exactly that kind of firm-specific event. Since Silver Lake, SWI's true marginal investor, held a concentrated ~75% stake and was not diversified, does the repo's regression beta of 0.88 understate the risk actually borne, because it implicitly assumes a diversified marginal investor that didn't exist here?
    - Sources needed: SWI weekly/monthly return regression vs. S&P 500 (beta + R²), evidence of whether Silver Lake actively traded shares or held a static position.
    - Where to find: REPO-SWI-VAL — Step 8 (regression beta 0.88) and Step 4 (marginal investor); DAMODARAN-SITE — total-beta/undiversified-investor adjustment methodology to re-run the calculation.
    - Answer: The 0.88 is understated, but for a mechanical reason, not an undiversified-investor reason — and total beta is the wrong fix. Silver Lake never held ~75%: it held 36.56% versus Thoma Bravo's 29.79% (swi-def14a-2024.md), and the sponsors traded steadily, taking combined ownership from 88.8% (12/31/2018) to 78.3% (12/31/2020) to 67.0% (12/31/2023) to 65.0% (12/31/2024) — roughly 24 points of the company sold down (swi-10k-fy2018.md, swi-10k-fy2020.md, swi-10k-fy2023.md, swi-10k-fy2024.md, Item 1A). What that leaves is a float of 11.2% at the start and 35.0% at the end, with the aggregate market value of non-affiliate shares only $661.3M as of June 28, 2024 (swi-10k-fy2024.md, cover page). A regression run on a stock that thin is biased *downward* by non-synchronous and illiquid trading — that alone explains 0.88 without invoking anyone's portfolio. Total beta (the repo's 4.57–4.60, implying a 25.5% cost of equity) is the tool for valuing a business being sold to a single undiversified owner, and here it fails a reality check: Goldman used a 10.3% cost of equity and a 9%–11% WACC, and Jefferies used a 10.70%–11.70% WACC, in the analyses that actually cleared the transaction (swi-defm14c-2025-merger-information-statement.md). Use the bottom-up levered beta of ~1.55; discard both 0.88 and 4.60.

- **Lesson 04, Session 4 · Part 3 — "Proxy Models and the Weakness of Beta — Does the CAPM Actually Work?"** ([transcript](../../01-foundations-and-discount-rates/lesson-04/session-4-part-3.md))
    - Quote: "The actual number is well below 10%."
    - Question for SWI: Given the repo's own regression beta (0.88) was flagged as low-to-moderate reliability, should a Fama-French-style proxy beta (using SWI's small-cap size and price-to-book at delisting) replace it, and would that produce a materially different cost of equity than the CAPM-only estimate already in the valuation file?
    - Sources needed: SWI market cap and price-to-book ratio at FY2024 year-end, Fama-French size/value factor return data.
    - Where to find: SEC-EDGAR — SWI 10-K FY2024 balance sheet (book equity) plus market cap from shares outstanding × price; DAMODARAN-SITE — for small-cap/proxy premium data; REPO-SWI-VAL — Step 8/9 for the existing beta baseline.
    - Answer: No — don't swap in a Fama-French proxy; fix the beta bottom-up instead. The inputs first: book equity was $1,400.714M on 171,566,604 shares = $8.16 book value per share (swi-10k-fy2024.md, consolidated balance sheets). The 12/31/2024 close isn't in the compiled sources, but it is derivable: Turn/River's $17.50–$18.00 December 27 offer was disclosed as a 25%–29% premium to the 30-day VWAP through December 24, 2024, which backs out to ~$14.00 (swi-defm14c-2025-merger-information-statement.md, "Background of the Merger"). That puts year-end market cap at ~$2.40B with P/B ≈ 1.72× and book-to-market ≈ 0.58 — genuinely small-cap (SWI joined the S&P SmallCap 600 in 2024) and genuinely a value stock, so a three-factor model would load positively on both SMB and HML. The problem is that Damodaran's own work says the size premium isn't there: he documents that it dissipated after 1981, vanishes outside January, vanishes when microcaps are excluded, and that his implied expected return on the S&P 600 SmallCap index was 7.61% — *below* the S&P 500 (03-4-blogs/posts/2015-04-11-the-small-cap-premium-where-is-the-beef.md). Do the arithmetic on the alternative anyway: bolting a conventional 3%–5% size premium onto the existing 11.75% cost of equity gives 14.75%–16.75% and a WACC of roughly 12.6%–14.2%, above the top of both banks' ranges and inconsistent with a deal that actually closed at $18.50. The low P/B is a pricing observation about a de-rated mature company, not a risk premium to be discounted with.

## Notes

Lesson 4 introduces the risk-and-return-model checklist and the ladder from firm-specific to market-wide risk, and applies both to SWI three different ways: does CAPM handle catastrophic tail risk, does a concentrated undiversified holder need a different beta, and does a Fama-French proxy beat CAPM for a de-rated small-cap. All three answers converge on the same fix: correct the beta bottom-up rather than reaching for a different model, because the premise driving each alternative-model argument — Silver Lake holding a concentrated, undiversified ~75% — is itself wrong. This is one of the lessons that most directly surfaces the ownership-concentration correction documented in [`README.md`](README.md) (Silver Lake ~36.6% and Thoma Bravo ~29.8%, sold down steadily, not a static ~75% Silver Lake bet), so later lessons should point back here rather than re-arguing the ownership split. It connects forward to Lessons 8–11, where the bottom-up beta this lesson argues for is actually built.
