# SolarWinds (SWI) — Lesson 03: The Objective: Reality and Reaction

### Lesson 03 — The Objective: Reality and Reaction

- **Lesson 03, Session 3 · Part 1 — "Intermediate Objectives — Why Market Share and Revenue Growth Are Not Enough"** ([transcript](../../01-foundations-and-discount-rates/lesson-03/session-3-part-1.md))
    - Quote: "Maximizing market share by itself is a dumb objective, and here's why."
    - Question for SolarWinds: Which headline metric does SolarWinds' management steer by (revenue, market share, recurring revenue, margin, earnings per share)? Is it a genuine proxy for value, or is it being hit in ways that damage value (discounting, bundling, under-investment)?
    - Sources needed: the metrics in the bonus and equity plans; ARR and retention; revenue by type; spending trends.
    - Where to find: ledger `governance.objectives`, `company.history`, `company.ebitda_adjusted`; `sources/filings/swi-10k-fy2024.md` (Item 7, Annual Recurring Revenue and net retention; operating expenses); `sources/filings/swi-def14a-2024.md` (Compensation Discussion and Analysis).
    - Answer: Management steers by Subscription ARR (annual recurring revenue from subscription contracts), revenue and adjusted EBITDA. The 2023 bonus plan paid 50% on revenue and 50% on adjusted EBITDA; the stock-unit awards paid 70% on Subscription ARR and 30% on revenue (`facts.pay-metrics-2023`). It is a partial proxy for value. Subscription ARR grew 33.7%, from $233.2M to $311.7M, while Total ARR grew only 6.6%, from $684.1M to $729.0M (`facts.subscription-arr-2024`, `facts.subscription-arr-2023`, `facts.total-arr-2024`, `facts.total-arr-2023`). Subtracting, the maintenance part of ARR fell by $33.6M. So $78.5M of subscription growth added only $44.8M of total recurring revenue: about 43% of the headline growth was existing customers converting from maintenance (arithmetic on the four facts). Net retention on subscriptions was 99% in 2024 and 97% in 2023 (`facts.net-retention-subscription`, `facts.net-retention-subscription`), so the installed subscription base roughly held its dollars rather than growing. Discounting or bundling: no filing quantifies either, so it cannot be tested. Under-investment: sales and marketing fell 9.8%, from $249.3M to $224.9M (`facts.sales-marketing-2024`, `facts.sales-marketing-2023`), of which $13.8M was a reclassification and $11.4M was cut marketing programs (`facts.sm-decrease-detail-2024`). The margin metric flatters: adjusted EBITDA was $384.7M, 48.3% of revenue (`company.ebitda_adjusted`), against $282.8M on the standardized basis the model uses (`company.ebitda_standardized`). The $101.9M gap is mostly $78.3M of stock-based pay and related payroll tax, which management adds back and the model treats as a cost (`modeling_choices.sbc_treatment`).

- **Lesson 03, Session 3 · Part 2 — "Self-Correcting Markets — Bond Covenants, Activist Investors, and the Imperial CEO"** ([transcript](../../01-foundations-and-discount-rates/lesson-03/session-3-part-2.md))
    - Quote: "Left to their own devices, markets figure out ways to punish companies that essentially violate the rules."
    - Question for SolarWinds: Has SolarWinds had a day of reckoning (a scandal, covenant breach, activist campaign, failed deal, or regulatory action)? Did markets, lenders or the board correct it, and did the correction leave a lasting mark on value or was it priced away?
    - Sources needed: SUNBURST chronology and costs; the SEC case; the CEO change; valuation multiples through time.
    - Where to find: `sources/filings/swi-8k-2020-12-09-sunburst-disclosure.md`, `swi-8k-2020-12-14-sunburst-followup.md`; `sources/filings/swi-10k-fy2024.md` (Item 1A, Note 15, Cyber Incident); `sources/deal-docs/swi-defm14c-2025-merger.md` (Goldman Sachs, historical multiples).
    - Answer: Yes: the SUNBURST attack on its Orion software, disclosed 2020-12-14. SolarWinds notified about 33,000 Orion customers and believed fewer than 18,000 had an affected installation (`facts.sunburst-customers`). Costs show in the filings: net Cyber Incident expense was $26.2M in 2022, a $2.1M net gain in 2023 after insurance proceeds, and $10.3M in 2024 (`facts.cyber-net-expense`). The securities class action settled for $26 million, paid by insurance (`facts.class-action-settlement`). The SEC sued the company and its security chief on 2023-10-30; the court dismissed most of the claims and one remained at the 2025-02-19 filing (`facts.sec-complaint`). Who corrected it: not the board in the way the question assumes. The CEO change was decided on 2020-12-07, a week before the attack was disclosed (`facts.ceo-change-2020-12-07`). No lender action and no activist campaign appear in the sources. The market's mark is large: investment banks' average enterprise value to next-twelve-months EBITDA multiple for SolarWinds fell from 15.9 times in 2019 and 15.7 times in 2020, above the software peers' 10.9 and 10.8, to 8.8 times in 2024 against peers' 12.8 (`facts.ev-ntm-ebitda-history`). Cause is not proven. The three bidders who withdrew in November 2023 gave "the relative early stage of the Company's ongoing transition to a subscription-first business model" as their reason (`facts.market-check-2023`), so the discount mixes SUNBURST with the transition and the 2021 spin-off. A $100 investment at the end of 2019 was worth $51 at the end of 2023 against $211 for the proxy's peer group (`facts.tsr-2023`). The $18.50 offer, a 21.9% premium to $15.18 (arithmetic), sits just above the 25th percentile in Goldman's study of software deals (25th percentile premium 19%, 75th percentile 50%) (`facts.goldman-premia-study`).

## Module 1 Step — Questions for This Lesson

- **Stated objectives**
  - If the firm states goals, what is it targeting (growth, profitability, stock price, value)? Growth and market position: "extend our leadership in observability and IT infrastructure management and grow our market share in adjacent areas", through a "subscription-first approach" (`governance.objectives`). It states no value or stock-price target.
  - If it states none, what does its behaviour say the primary focus is? It states goals, but behavior adds a second one: operating profit. Pay is tied to revenue, adjusted EBITDA and Subscription ARR (`facts.pay-metrics-2023`), sales and marketing was cut 9.8%, and the company paid $168.2M to stockholders in 2024 (`company.history`). The focus is recurring-revenue growth and cash margin, not value per share.

## Notes

The metrics are real but flattering: the ARR growth rate is mostly conversion, and the margin metric excludes stock pay. Lesson 14 will measure return on capital without those adjustments. The de-rating is a lasting mark, but the filings give three possible causes, so lesson 28 should not attribute the whole gap to SUNBURST. On the SEC case, the 10-K says the court dismissed most claims and one remained, which is what is recorded here. Lesson 35 reuses the deal premium.
