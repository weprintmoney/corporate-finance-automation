# SolarWinds (SWI) — Lesson 32: Terminal Value

### Lesson 32 — Terminal Value

- **Lesson 32, Session 32 · Part 1 — "Capping the Growth Rate: How Long Should the Growth Period Last?"** ([transcript](../../04-dividends-and-valuation/lesson-32/session-32-part-1.md))
    - Quote: "The bigger your competitive advantages and the more sustainable they are, the longer your growth period can be."
    - Question for SolarWinds: How durable are SolarWinds' competitive advantages (switching costs, brand, network, cost position), and how long a growth period do they justify before the company is treated as mature? What evidence (retention, share, margins versus peers) supports the choice?
    - Sources needed: Retention and customer-count disclosures; revenue and margin history; peer growth and margins; the buyers' 2023 market check.
    - Where to find: `sources/filings/swi-10k-fy2024.md` MD&A (net retention rate, Total ARR, customers above $100,000); `company.history`; `peers`; `sources/deal-docs/swi-defm14c-2025-merger.md` Background of the Merger.
    - Answer: Six years, 2025 to 2030 (`forecast.growth_period_years` = 6), which is management's own forecast window. The moat is real but it defends, it does not expand. For:
        - Retention. Subscription net retention was 99% in 2024 and 97% in 2023 (`facts.net-retention-subscription`, `facts.net-retention-subscription`). Customers kept what they bought and bought almost nothing more.
        - Stickiness. Customers with more than $100,000 of annual recurring revenue rose from 979 to 1,148 (`facts.customers-over-100k-arr`). Total ARR was $729.0M, up 6.6% (`facts.total-arr-2024`). There was no exodus after the 2020 SUNBURST attack: revenue went 719.4, 758.7, 796.9 ($M, `company.history`), about 5.0% in 2024.
        - Margins. GAAP EBIT margin is 26.2% ($208.4M ÷ $796.9M, `company.ebit`, `company.revenue`) against a peer median of 15.9% (median of `peers[].ebit_margin`, my arithmetic). Customers pay for software they do not leave, which is what a switching-cost moat looks like.
        - Against a longer period. Growth is 5.0%, close to the peer median of 4.1% (`peers[].growth`), so there is no share gain. In the 2023 market check, three sponsors that had signed on dropped out in November 2023, citing the early stage of the subscription transition and doubt that it would succeed (deal document, Background of the Merger). Three bidders gave price indications. The highest, from "Party A", was $13.00 a share, revised down from about $14.00; Party B indicated $12.00 to $13.00 and Party C $12.50, and all three withdrew (`facts.market-check-2023`).
        - Against a shorter one. Management's plan has revenue growing 8.12% a year (`forecast.management_growth`) and EBIT margin rising from 26.2% to 40.14% by 2030 (`forecast.years`). A shorter period would cut the margin ramp. We did not cut it because the plan is the status-quo story and the course asks us to value that first.
        - Judgment call. Six years, with the plan's growth treated as optimistic against a 5.0% history. A shorter period is arguable; a longer one has no support in the retention data.

- **Lesson 32, Session 32 · Part 2 — "Valuing a Mature Company and Avoiding the Terminal Value Inconsistency Trap"** ([transcript](../../04-dividends-and-valuation/lesson-32/session-32-part-2.md))
    - Quote: "If your net CapEx is zero, and your change in working capital is zero, you're reinvesting nothing, right?"
    - Question for SolarWinds: In SolarWinds' terminal year, what reinvestment rate is consistent with the perpetual growth rate and the stable return on capital? Does the forecast avoid the trap of capital spending that exactly offsets depreciation while growth continues?
    - Sources needed: Terminal growth rate, stable ROIC, management's 2030 cash flow and EBIT, capex and D&A history.
    - Where to find: `market.terminal_growth`, `forecast.stable_roic`, `forecast.years`, `company.capex`, `company.da`, `company.acquisition_amortization`; model-output.md §5 and §6.
    - Answer: The consistent reinvestment rate is 20.83% (g ÷ stable ROIC = 2.5% ÷ 12%, model-output.md §6). The forecast does not avoid the trap.
        - Stable-period inputs. Stable growth firms have betas closer to one and debt ratios closer to the industry average or their optimum, and their excess returns shrink (lesson 32 slides). So the terminal value uses stable beta 1.00 (`forecast.stable_beta`), debt 21% (`forecast.stable_debt_ratio`, SolarWinds' optimum from §3) and stable ROIC 12% (`forecast.stable_roic`), 3.5 points above the stable cost of capital; the project answer below gives the reasons. Cost of equity is 4.43% + 1.00 × 4.92% = 9.35%; the after-tax cost of debt stays 5.33% (7.11% pre-tax, held); the stable cost of capital is 0.79 × 9.35% + 0.21 × 5.33% = 8.51% (§6, my arithmetic). The 2025–2030 cash flows are still discounted at 10.23%; the terminal value is capitalized at 8.51% and discounted back at 10.23%.
        - Management's 2030 unlevered free cash flow (FCFF) after stock compensation is $419.0M (`forecast.years`). EBIT in 2030 is $511M (1,273.0 × 40.14%), or $383.3M after 25% tax. FCFF above after-tax EBIT means net reinvestment of about −$35.7M, a reinvestment rate near −9% (my arithmetic: 419.0 − 383.3 = 35.7). The gap comes from a forecast +$46M change in deferred revenue and cash taxes near 25% of EBIT (`facts.forecast-2030-detail`). It does not come from depreciation running ahead of capital spending: in 2030 D&A is $25M and capex $27M.
        - The model's terminal value, with consistent reinvestment, is $5,177.6M. Growing the final-year FCFF at g instead gives $7,150.4M (model-output.md §6). The difference is $1,972.8M at 2030.
        - Discounted at the 2030 factor of 0.5573 and divided by 171.6M shares, that difference is about $6.41 a share ($23.86 − $17.45, §6). The status-quo DCF is $17.45 (§6); with the plan's terminal cash flow it would be $23.86 (§6, "value per share with the other terminal value"), well above the $18.50 deal price. That value is what the plan is worth if cash flow can grow forever with no reinvestment.
        - The stable return does move value, but it takes a lasting advantage to reach the deal price. The model's sensitivity (§6) gives value per share of $15.63 at 8.51% (no excess returns), $16.56 at 10%, $17.45 at 12% (ledger), $18.33 at 15%, $19.22 at 20% and $20.10 at 30%. Our range runs from $15.63 to $18.33. The $18.50 deal price needs a stable return of roughly 16% forever, and even a 30% return on new capital stays below $23.86. The final-year-FCFF terminal value implies terminal reinvestment of −9.33%, so no positive return on capital reaches it; only negative reinvestment forever does.
        - The capex and D&A trap shows up in the history too. 2024 capex was $20.5M against D&A of $74.4M, net capex of −$53.9M (model-output.md §5). Of that D&A, $52.9M is amortization of intangibles acquired in the 2016 buyout (`company.acquisition_amortization`), a non-cash charge. The gap is not hidden shrinkage of the operating base, but it means the base-year cash flow flatters a company that reinvests little. The terminal year must not copy it.

## Cumulative Project — Questions for This Lesson

- **When (in what year) will your firm be a mature firm (where you can estimated terminal value)?**
  2030, the last year of management's forecast (`forecast.growth_period_years` = 6). SolarWinds already grows near 5.0%, so "mature" here means the point where we stop trusting the plan's margin ramp, not a sudden change in the business.

- **What will your firm's growth rate be in perpetuity after that point in time?**
  2.5% (`market.terminal_growth`). It sits below the 4.43% risk-free rate (`market.risk_free_rate`), the cap the lecture sets, and between Goldman Sachs' 2%–3% and Jefferies' 2.5%–3.5% perpetuity ranges (`facts.gs-dcf-range`, `facts.jefferies-dcf-range`).

- **What return on and cost of capital would you give your firm, when it becomes a mature firm?**
  Cost of capital 8.51% in the stable period, return on capital 12% (`forecast.stable_roic`), reinvestment 20.83% (2.5% ÷ 12%) (model-output.md §6). The stable cost of capital resets the two inputs the lecture says change at maturity. Beta moves to 1.0 (`forecast.stable_beta`), because stable growth firms have betas closer to one, which gives a cost of equity of 4.43% + 1.00 × 4.92% = 9.35%. Debt moves to 21% (`forecast.stable_debt_ratio`), SolarWinds' optimum from §3, because stable firms move toward the industry average or their optimum; the after-tax cost of debt is held at 5.33%. The blend is 8.51%. The 2025–2030 years keep 10.23% (§1), and the terminal value is discounted back at that rate. The return on capital is the judgment, and 12% is 3.5 points above the stable cost of capital. Why not 8.51%, the no-excess-returns case: SolarWinds earned 6.66% on book invested capital in 2024, and 6.53% with R&D capitalized (model-output.md §8 "R&D capitalized"), but $2,363.2M of that capital is goodwill from the 2016 buyout (`company.goodwill`), so the book return measures the price the sponsors paid, not the business. On the $191.0M of operating capital excluding goodwill the return is 87.35% (60.56% on a 5-year R&D life). The business needs almost no capital, keeps about 99% of its subscription revenue (`facts.net-retention-subscription`) and raises prices every year (`forecast.stable_roic` source), so new capital should earn more than it costs. Why not 87%: the license base is shrinking and the observability rivals grow faster (`forecast.stable_roic` source; license revenue is down to 6.5% of revenue, `facts.license-revenue`), so the excess return fades toward the cost of capital rather than holding at today's level. Damodaran's Disney stable return carried a 2.7-point spread over its stable cost of capital (`forecast.stable_roic` source). The §6 sensitivity shows the stakes: $15.63 at 8.51%, $16.56 at the course's 10% convention, $17.45 at 12%.

## Notes

Lesson 32 sets the terminal value, which is 69% of the DCF enterprise value ($2,885.4M of $4,207.0M, model-output.md §6). The six-year period and 2.5% growth are inputs; the large judgment is the stable return on capital (12%, `forecast.stable_roic`) and the terminal reinvestment rate it sets (20.83%). The ledger's `terminal_method: consistent_reinvestment` replaces the shortcut of growing the final-year cash flow, which assumes no reinvestment in perpetuity. Lesson 34 uses the $17.45 this produces, and lesson 36 uses the $6.41 gap and the return sensitivity to explain the deal price.

Ledger note: `forecast.wacc_stable` is not a ledger field; the model derives the stable cost of capital (8.51%) in §6 from `forecast.stable_beta`, `forecast.stable_debt_ratio` and the held cost of debt.
