# Colgate-Palmolive (CL) — Lesson 02: The Objective: Utopia and Let Down

### Lesson 02 — The Objective: Utopia and Let Down

- **Lesson 02, Session 2 · Part 1 — "The Four Linkages Behind the Utopian World of Stock Price Maximization"** ([transcript](../../01-foundations-and-discount-rates/lesson-02/session-2-part-1.md))
    - Quote: "Finally, in this Utopian world, there are no social costs or social benefits."
    - Question for Colgate-Palmolive: For each of the four linkages, where is Colgate-Palmolive weakest? In particular, how are its lenders protected (covenants, change-of-control terms), and has any recent event tested that protection?
    - Sources needed: Board and ownership; debt terms and rating history; legal matters.
    - Where to find: ledger `governance.*`, `debt.covenants`, `debt.rating_actual`, `facts.sp-rating-action`, `facts.legal-matters-2025`, `facts.executive-pay-2025`, `facts.free-cash-flow-productivity-2023-2025`; `sources/filings/cl-10k-fy2025.txt` notes 6 and 13; `sources/capital-iq/credit-ratings.tsv`.
    - Answer: Managers and stockholders: sound on paper, weakest in how pay treats failed acquisitions. Nine of ten nominees are independent and directors face an annual majority vote (`facts.board-structure-2026`). But the earnings measure that drives 80% of the bonus excludes special items, and the pay committee restated free cash flow productivity from 133.6% to 115.7% by taking the write-down and the ERISA charge out of net income (`facts.executive-pay-2025`, `facts.free-cash-flow-productivity-2023-2025`). So the $919M skin health write-down cost shareholders and did not cost management's incentive pay. Lenders: this is the weakest link. Bank agreements carry financial covenants and cross-default terms, and the company says it complies (`debt.covenants`). The public notes are what most lenders hold, and the debt note mentions covenants only for the bank borrowings. The event that tested lender protection: S&P's action of 2024-04-19, recorded by Capital IQ as a downgrade to A+ (`facts.sp-rating-action`). Our files do not give S&P's reasons, so the link to buybacks is an inference: Colgate returned $9,438M in three years against free cash flow to equity of $8,204M and holds book equity of $54M (model-output.md §5; `company.equity_book`). Markets: the strongest link. Twenty analysts publish price targets (`facts.consensus-target-price`) and 2,356 institutions hold the stock (`facts.holders-capital-iq`). Society: real but small next to the firm. There are 454 talcum powder cases pending, up from 308, and the ERISA pension suit was settled for $332M; the company puts reasonably possible losses above accruals at $0 to about $225M (`facts.legal-matters-2025`).

- **Lesson 02, Session 2 · Part 2 — "Golden Parachutes, Poison Pills, and Managerial Self-Interest at Shareholder Expense"** ([transcript](../../01-foundations-and-discount-rates/lesson-02/session-2-part-2.md))
    - Quote: "Given a choice between their interest and what's in the best interest of stockholders, managers often put their interest over stockholder interests."
    - Question for Colgate-Palmolive: What do Colgate-Palmolive's executive pay and change-of-control agreements reward: closing a deal, the share price, or long-run value? Are there golden parachutes, poison pills, or other devices that protect managers rather than stockholders?
    - Sources needed: Pay design; severance and change-in-control terms; takeover defenses.
    - Where to find: ledger `facts.executive-pay-2025`, `facts.change-in-control-terms`, `facts.governance-provisions`; `sources/filings/cl-def14a-2026.txt` (Compensation Discussion and Analysis; Potential Payments Upon Termination or Change in Control); `sources/capital-iq/corporate-governance.tsv`.
    - Answer: Pay rewards growth and earnings first, the share price second. The annual bonus is 80% earnings per share (before special items) and organic sales growth. The three-year share award is 50% organic sales growth against peers, 30% net income growth against peers and 20% free cash flow productivity, then moved up or down by as much as 25% on shareholder return against peers (`facts.executive-pay-2025`). Nothing in it measures return on the capital put into acquisitions, which is the one place value was lost. Change of control: there is a golden parachute, and it is a restrained one. Payments need two triggers, a change in control and then a qualifying termination within two years. Mr. Wallace would receive about $45.3M, of which $25.3M is performance share units, 0.06% of the equity value (`facts.change-in-control-terms`). A payment that size does not tilt a $78.5 billion decision. There is no poison pill and no classified board; stockholders holding 15% can call a special meeting and can act by written consent (`facts.governance-provisions`). The devices that protect managers are softer: the chief executive also chairs the board, and the pay measures leave out the charges that record past mistakes.

- **Lesson 02, Session 2 · Part 3 — "Where the Power Lies — Reading Top Shareholders to Judge Minority Protection"** ([transcript](../../01-foundations-and-discount-rates/lesson-02/session-2-part-3.md))
    - Quote: "Institutional investors, when they don't like the way a company is run, tend to sell their shares and move on."
    - Question for Colgate-Palmolive: Who are Colgate-Palmolive's top shareholders, and what protections exist for minority holders (independent committees, majority-of-minority votes, limits on controlling votes)? Do the institutional holders fight when they disagree, or just sell?
    - Sources needed: 5% holders; holder types; activist presence; record of stockholder proposals.
    - Where to find: ledger `governance.controlling_holders`, `governance.share_classes`, `facts.holders-capital-iq`, `facts.institutional-ownership`, `facts.insiders-pct-proxy`, `facts.board-structure-2026`; `sources/filings/cl-def14a-2026.txt`; `sources/capital-iq/public-ownership.tsv`.
    - Answer: Vanguard 10.26%, BlackRock 8.18% and State Street 5.95% (`governance.controlling_holders`). All three mostly run index funds. There is no controlling holder, so every holder is a minority holder and the usual protections against a controller do not arise. One share, one vote (`governance.share_classes`). Institutions hold 87.19% and directors and officers 0.43% (`facts.institutional-ownership`, `facts.insiders-pct-proxy`). Fight or sell? The largest holders can do neither in the usual sense: an index fund cannot sell, and it rarely leads a campaign. No activist holds a meaningful stake; the largest holder Capital IQ tags as an activist owns 0.05% (`facts.holders-capital-iq`). What exists is slow pressure by vote. The special-meeting threshold came down from 25% to 15% in 2021 after a stockholder proposal, the severance-ratification policy followed in 2022, and a proposal for an independent chairman is on the 2026 ballot with the board against it (`facts.board-structure-2026`, `facts.change-in-control-terms`). Vote results are not in our files, so how close these votes ran is unknown.

## Module 1 Step — Questions for This Lesson

- **Corporate governance**
  - Who sits on the board of directors? Ten nominees: Noel Wallace, who is Chairman, President and chief executive, and nine independent directors led by Lorrie Norrington as lead independent director (`governance.board`). Average tenure of the independent nominees is 8.1 years and the retirement age is 72 (`facts.board-structure-2026`).
  - Whose interests are they most likely to serve? Stockholders in form, management in drift. No director represents a large holder, because there is none. The directors are independent by the exchange's test, but the person who sets their agenda is the executive they supervise, and the pay they approve leaves write-downs out of the measures.
  - Where does power lie, and what measure of governance strength supports that? With management, held in check by a board that stockholders can replace every year. Supporting measures: annual majority-vote elections, a 15% special-meeting right, action by written consent, no poison pill, no classified board, bylaws amendable by a 50% vote (`facts.governance-provisions`, `facts.board-structure-2026`). Against: a combined chairman and chief executive, and owners who are mostly index funds.

## Notes

Colgate is the textbook dispersed-ownership company: strong formal rights, no owner with the incentive to use them. That matters for the valuation in two ways. First, nobody has forced the questions the model raises: debt at 9.34% against a 30% optimum (model-output.md §3), and $2,211M of write-downs. Second, the price is set by diversified institutions, which is what lets lesson 4 use a market beta. Confidence: high on the facts, which are disclosed. The statement that the 2024 downgrade tested lenders is an inference; S&P's reasoning is not in `sources/`.
