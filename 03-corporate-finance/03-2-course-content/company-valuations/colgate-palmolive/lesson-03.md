# Colgate-Palmolive (CL) — Lesson 03: The Objective: Reality and Reaction

### Lesson 03 — The Objective: Reality and Reaction

- **Lesson 03, Session 3 · Part 1 — "Intermediate Objectives — Why Market Share and Revenue Growth Are Not Enough"** ([transcript](../../01-foundations-and-discount-rates/lesson-03/session-3-part-1.md))
    - Quote: "Maximizing market share by itself is a dumb objective, and here's why."
    - Question for Colgate-Palmolive: Which headline metric does Colgate-Palmolive's management steer by (revenue, market share, recurring revenue, margin, earnings per share)? Is it a genuine proxy for value, or is it being hit in ways that damage value (discounting, bundling, under-investment)?
    - Sources needed: Stated objectives; pay measures; price and volume split of growth; advertising and margin history.
    - Where to find: ledger `governance.objectives`, `facts.management-indicators`, `facts.executive-pay-2025`, `facts.sales-drivers-2025`, `facts.growth-and-margin-history`, `facts.uses-of-cash-2025`; `module-1-cross-checks-output.md` §6; `sources/filings/cl-10k-fy2025.txt` MD&A.
    - Answer: Two metrics: organic sales growth and earnings per share before special items. Both are in the stated objectives (`governance.objectives`) and both drive pay (`facts.executive-pay-2025`). Organic sales growth is net sales growth with currency moves and acquisitions taken out. It is a fair proxy for value only if the growth is profitable, and the record says it has been getting less so. From 2016 to 2025 net sales grew 3.3% a year and operating income before write-downs grew 0.7% a year; the margin fell from 24.0% in 2017 to 20.7% in 2025 (`facts.growth-and-margin-history`). Advertising rose from $1,573M in 2017 to $2,703M in 2025 (`module-1-cross-checks-output.md` §6). So the growth target was met by spending more to get each dollar of sales. That is not discounting; it is the opposite risk. In 2025 prices rose 2.1% and volume fell 0.4% (`facts.sales-drivers-2025`), so growth is being held up by price while fewer units are sold. Under-investment is not the problem: capital spending of $564M is 2.8% of sales and the return on capital is 34.56% (model-output.md §8). The earnings measure has its own flaw. It leaves out write-downs, restructuring and litigation, and those are not rare: write-downs in 2021, 2022 and 2025, and one restructuring program ending in 2024 as the next began in 2025 (`facts.writedowns-2021-2025`, `facts.restructuring-program-2025`). Our base operating income keeps restructuring and litigation in for that reason. The honest reading: the metrics are real, and they are being hit in a way that has added sales faster than value.

- **Lesson 03, Session 3 · Part 2 — "Self-Correcting Markets — Bond Covenants, Activist Investors, and the Imperial CEO"** ([transcript](../../01-foundations-and-discount-rates/lesson-03/session-3-part-2.md))
    - Quote: "Left to their own devices, markets figure out ways to punish companies that essentially violate the rules."
    - Question for Colgate-Palmolive: Has Colgate-Palmolive had a day of reckoning (a scandal, covenant breach, activist campaign, failed deal, or regulatory action)? Did markets, lenders or the board correct it, and did the correction leave a lasting mark on value or was it priced away?
    - Sources needed: Write-downs, rating actions, litigation, shareholder return against peers and the market, activist holdings.
    - Where to find: ledger `facts.writedowns-2021-2025`, `facts.sp-rating-action`, `facts.legal-matters-2025`, `facts.shareholder-return-2020-2025`, `facts.holders-capital-iq`, `facts.executive-pay-2025`, `facts.consensus-target-price`; `sources/filings/cl-def14a-2026.txt` (Pay Versus Performance).
    - Answer: No single day of reckoning: no scandal, no covenant breach, no activist campaign. What Colgate has had is a slow one. Failed deals: $2,211M of write-downs in five years (`facts.writedowns-2021-2025`). Lenders: a downgrade to A+ in April 2024 (`facts.sp-rating-action`). Courts: the ERISA settlement of $332M (`facts.legal-matters-2025`). The stock market delivered the verdict. $100 put into Colgate at the end of 2020 was worth $104.0 at the end of 2025, against $111.8 for the proxy's peer group, and over the same five years the S&P 500 returned 96.2% (`facts.shareholder-return-2020-2025`). Who corrected what? The pay plan responded a little: the 2025 bonus paid 93.6% of target (`facts.executive-pay-2025`). The board's visible response is a new strategy and a new cost program, not a change in who runs the company or how deals are judged. No outside investor has forced more; no activist holds even a tenth of one percent (`facts.holders-capital-iq`). Lasting mark or priced away? The five flat years are the mark. On the analysis date the stock is at $98.71, close to the analysts' average target of $101.70 (`company.share_price`, `facts.consensus-target-price`), so the market is not pricing a turnaround. Lessons 30 to 32 test whether that price already assumes too much.

## Module 1 Step — Questions for This Lesson

- **Stated objectives**
  - If the firm states goals, what is it targeting (growth, profitability, stock price, value)? Growth first, then earnings, efficiency and cash: "driving organic sales growth; delivering consistent, compounded earnings per share growth; achieving operational efficiencies", in order to "deliver peer-leading performance and total shareholder return" (`governance.objectives`). Shareholder return is named as the end; growth and earnings per share are the means it is managed by. Value, in the sense of returns above the cost of capital, is not a stated target, though return on capital is on the list of indicators management watches (`facts.management-indicators`).
  - If it states none, what does its behaviour say the primary focus is? It states them. Behaviour adds one thing the statement leaves out: the steadiest commitment is the payout. Dividends rose to $2.06 a share and a $5 billion buyback was authorized in a year when net sales grew 1.4% (`facts.payout-policy-2025`, `facts.sales-drivers-2025`).

## Notes

Colgate steers by growth and adjusted earnings, and neither has tracked value well over the last nine years: sales up 3.3% a year, operating income up 0.7%. That gap is the main thing the forecast in lessons 30 to 32 has to take a view on, because analysts expect the margin to rise from here (`forecast.years`). The market's correction has been mild and slow, which fits a company with no large owner. Confidence: high on the numbers. The claim that growth was "bought" with advertising is our reading of two disclosed series moving together, not something the filing says.
