# Colgate-Palmolive (CL) — Lesson 24: Trends and Measures

### Lesson 24 — Trends and Measures

Lesson 24 has no lecture transcript in the repo, so there are no transcript-layer entries. The two project questions are answered from the ledger. The slides define the measures: the payout ratio is dividends divided by net income; the dividend yield is dividends per share divided by the share price.

## Cumulative Project — Questions for This Lesson

- **How much (if any) has your company paid out in dividends each year for the last 5 years?**
  $8,731M over 2021 to 2025, rising every year (`facts.dividends-2021-2025`). Amounts in $ millions; per-share figures in dollars.

  | Year | Dividends paid | Per share | Buybacks | Dividends plus buybacks |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 2021 | 1,679 | 1.79 | 1,320 | 2,999 |
  | 2022 | 1,691 | 1.86 | 1,308 | 2,999 |
  | 2023 | 1,749 | 1.91 | 1,128 | 2,877 |
  | 2024 | 1,789 | 1.98 | 1,739 | 3,528 |
  | 2025 | 1,823 | 2.06 | 1,210 | 3,033 |
  | Five years | 8,731 | | 6,705 | 15,436 |

  The pattern is the one the lecture describes for mature companies. The dividend is regular, quarterly, and raised a little each year: the total paid grew 2.1% a year and the amount per share 3.6% a year, the difference being the shrinking share count. In 2025 the quarterly dividend went from $0.50 to $0.52 (`facts.payout-policy-2025`). Buybacks are the flexible part, moving between $1,128M and $1,739M. Together they have returned about $3 billion every year.
  One reading note: the 10-K's statement of shareholders' equity shows $2.48 a share declared in 2024 and $2.08 in 2025. That is timing, not a cut: five dividends were declared in 2024 and four in 2025. Dividends paid per share rose from $1.98 to $2.06.

- **What dividend yield and payout ratio does this translate into?**

  | Year | Net income $M | Payout ratio | Diluted earnings per share | December share price | Dividend yield | Dividends plus buybacks as % of net income |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 2021 | 2,166 | 77.52% | 2.55 | 85.34 | 2.10% | 138.5% |
  | 2022 | 1,785 | 94.73% | 2.13 | 78.79 | 2.36% | 168.0% |
  | 2023 | 2,300 | 76.04% | 2.77 | 79.71 | 2.40% | 125.1% |
  | 2024 | 2,889 | 61.92% | 3.51 | 90.91 | 2.18% | 122.1% |
  | 2025 | 2,132 | 85.51% | 2.63 | 79.02 | 2.61% | 142.3% |
  | Five years | 11,272 | 77.46% | | | | 136.9% |

  Payout ratio: 77.46% over the five years, between 61.92% and 94.73% in single years. The swings come from net income, not from the dividend. The two highest years, 2022 and 2025, are write-down years, and so is 2021. With the 2025 write-down's $794M after-tax cost added back to net income, the 2025 payout is 62.3%, in line with 2024. A fair description is that Colgate pays out a little over 60% of its earnings before write-downs as dividends.
  Dividend yield: 2.10% to 2.61% at year-end prices. Today it is 2.11%: four payments of $0.52 make $2.08 a share on a price of $98.71. The yield fell from 2.61% at the end of 2025 because the share price rose from $79.02, not because anything changed in the dividend.
  The fuller measure counts buybacks. Colgate returned 136.9% of its net income over the five years. On today's equity value the dividend is 2.32% and buybacks 1.54%, 3.87% together (`facts.dividends-2021-2025`). Returning more than net income is possible without borrowing because Colgate's cash flow exceeds its net income: write-downs reduce earnings but not cash, and capital spending is below depreciation (lesson 14). Lesson 26 tests the cash return against free cash flow to equity, which is the right yardstick.

## Notes

The record: a regular dividend that rises 2% to 4% a year, a payout of about 77% of reported net income (about 60% before write-downs), a yield of about 2.1%, and buybacks that take the total cash returned to about $3 billion a year. All figures are disclosed; the only arithmetic is division. Confidence: high.

The per-share dividends come from Capital IQ's income statement page and agree with the 10-K for 2024 and 2025 (`facts.payout-policy-2025`). The yields use December closing prices from the price file, not the price on each payment date.

For lesson 25: this is a company whose shareholders have been trained to expect a dividend that never falls. For lesson 28: the industry averages a 67.25% payout and a 2.94% yield.
