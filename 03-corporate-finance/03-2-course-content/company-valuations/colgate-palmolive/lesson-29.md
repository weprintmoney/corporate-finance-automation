# Colgate-Palmolive (CL) — Lesson 29: First Steps

### Lesson 29 — First Steps

Lessons 29 to 35 value Colgate. The valuation itself runs in the professor's `fcffsimpleginzu.xlsx` (`professor-spreadsheet/results.md`). `model-output.md` section 6 is a cross-check on a three-year consensus forecast and is labelled that way wherever it is quoted. Arithmetic that neither of them holds is in `valuation-cross-checks-output.md`, which is generated by a script kept beside it.

- **Lesson 29, Session 29 · Part 1 — "Three Ways to Value a Company and the Four Questions Behind Every DCF"** ([transcript](../../04-dividends-and-valuation/lesson-29/session-29-part-1.md))
    - Quote: "When you have shifting, unstable, or unpredictable debt ratios, you should be valuing the entire firm."
    - Question for Colgate-Palmolive: Given how Colgate-Palmolive's debt ratio has moved over the years in the ledger, should it be valued as a firm (cash flows before debt payments) or as equity (cash flows after them)? What four inputs will the DCF need, and which is the hardest to estimate here?
    - Sources needed: Debt ratio by year; the debt recommendation; book equity; the valuation inputs.
    - Where to find: ledger `facts.debt-ratio-history`, `company.equity_book`, `facts.ginzu-inputs`, `facts.ginzu-sensitivities`; lessons 19 and 22.
    - Answer: As a firm. The quote's test does not force that choice here: Colgate's debt ratio has been steady, between 9.83% and 12.40% at each year end from 2020 to 2025 with no trend (`facts.debt-ratio-history`), and 9.34% today. A company with debt that steady could be valued through its equity cash flows. Three things still point to valuing the whole firm.
        - We recommend a change in the debt ratio, to 20% (lesson 19). An equity valuation would need a forecast of borrowing and repayment year by year; a firm valuation only needs a different cost of capital.
        - Book equity is $54M (`company.equity_book`), so every equity measure built on it, return on equity above all, is unusable (lesson 14). The firm measures work.
        - The professor's valuation workbook values the firm, and the project asks for it.
      The alternative, discounting free cash flow to equity at the cost of equity, would be a legitimate cross-check for a company this stable. We did not run it.
      The four inputs every discounted cash flow valuation (DCF: the value today of the cash a business will produce) needs:

        | Input | For Colgate | How hard |
        |---|---|---|
        | Cash flow from what the company already owns | Operating income of $4,080M over the last twelve months, after tax, less reinvestment | Moderate: the one judgment is which charges to keep in (lesson 30) |
        | Growth | 2.25% next year, 3.1% a year after | Easy: history, analysts and management agree within a point (lesson 31) |
        | Discount rate | 8.34% | Moderate: the beta range moves value by about $3 (lesson 30) |
        | When the company is mature, and what it earns after that | Mature now; the question is the return on new investment and the growth rate, forever | Hardest (lesson 32) |

      The hardest input is the last one. Colgate earns 34.56% on its capital today against a cost of 8.34% (model-output.md §8). How much of that advantage survives on new investments made ten and twenty years from now cannot be observed. The answer moves value from $51.84 a share (no advantage on new investment) to $64.90 (today's advantage kept), with growth held at 3% (`facts.ginzu-sensitivities`). Add the choice of the growth rate itself and the range widens to $50.90 to $79.74 (`facts.ginzu-base-case-detail`).

## Cumulative Project — Questions for This Lesson

- **What is the market capitalization (market value of equity) for your company, relative to its earnings and cash flows?**
  $78,466.6M on 2026-11-20: 794.9 million shares at $98.71 (`company.equity_value`). Against the 2025 figures (`facts.market-value-history`):

  | Measure | 2025 amount $M | Market value as a multiple |
  |---|---|---|
  | Net income as reported | 2,132 | 36.8 |
  | Net income with the write-down added back after tax | 2,926 | 26.8 |
  | Analysts' 2026 net income before exceptional items | 3,152.68 | 24.9 |
  | Free cash flow to equity (FCFE) | 2,712 | 28.9 |
  | Free cash flow to the firm (FCFF) | 3,579 | 21.9 |

  The reported multiple of 36.8 overstates how expensive the shares are, because 2025 net income carries a $794M after-tax write-down that took no cash. On the cleaner measures the market pays 25 to 29 times a year's earnings or equity cash flow. Put the other way, a buyer at today's price gets a cash yield of about 3.5% (3.46%, `facts.valuation-arithmetic`) from a business whose sales have grown about 3% a year. Those two numbers together are below the 8.76% an investor in Colgate's shares should require (model-output.md §1). That is the first sign, before any valuation, that the price asks a lot.

- **How much has that market capitalization changed over time?**
  Little over six years, and a lot in the last eleven months (`facts.market-value-history`).

  | Year end | Market value $M | Net income $M | Multiple of net income |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 2020 | 72,674 | 2,695 | |
  | 2021 | 71,727 | 2,166 | 33.1 |
  | 2022 | 65,412 | 1,785 | 36.6 |
  | 2023 | 65,475 | 2,300 | 28.5 |
  | 2024 | 73,871 | 2,889 | 25.6 |
  | 2025 | 63,314 | 2,132 | 29.7 |
  | 2026-11-20 | 78,467 | 2,132 | 36.8 |

  From the end of 2020 the market value rose 8.0%, or 1.3% a year. The share price did better, up 15.4% from $85.51, because buybacks cut the share count by 6.5%. Year-end values ranged from $63,314M (2025) to $73,871M (2024). Then the value rose 23.9% between the end of 2025 and 2026-11-20. Over those same months operating income before write-downs went from $4,225M for 2025 to $4,080M for the twelve months to September 2026 (`facts.ginzu-inputs`). The price rose while the earnings behind it slipped.

- **How have earnings/cash flows changed over time?**
  Sales grew steadily; profit grew slowly; reported net income fell (`facts.growth-history-and-consensus`, `facts.fcff-fcfe-2021-2025`).
    - Net sales: $16,471M in 2020 and $20,382M in 2025, 4.4% a year. Over the longer stretch from 2016, 3.3% a year.
    - Operating income before write-downs: $3,885M in 2020 and $4,225M in 2025, 1.7% a year; 0.7% a year from 2016.
    - Net income: $2,695M in 2020 and $2,132M in 2025, down 20.9%. With the 2025 write-down added back it is up 8.6% over five years, 1.7% a year.
    - FCFF: $2,774M in 2021 and $3,579M in 2025, up 29.0%, helped by working capital releasing cash in each of the last three years.
    - FCFE: between $2,314M and $2,885M in every year from 2021 to 2025. Steady.
  So the market value rose 8.0% over a period in which underlying profit rose about as much. Until 2026 the price and the earnings moved together. The 2026 rise is the part earnings do not explain.

## Notes

What this lesson establishes: Colgate is valued as a whole firm; the price is 25 to 29 times cleaned-up earnings or equity cash flow; and the value will turn on what happens after the forecast years, not during them.

Confidence: high on the market value, earnings and cash flow history, all from filings and prices. The observation that the 2026 price rise outran earnings is a fact; why it happened is not in our files, and we do not guess. One piece of context from the price file: the S&P 500 fell 8.8% in the first three weeks of November 2026 while Colgate rose 3.6% (`facts.sp500-november-2026`), which is how a low-beta staple behaves in a falling market.

Used later: the cash flow tables feed lesson 30; the growth history feeds lesson 31; the range of values is settled in lessons 32 to 34.
