## Cumulative Project — Questions for This Lesson

- **Is your firm under, over or correctly levered?**
  In regulatory terms, over-capitalized against today's requirement by $54,098M (2.6 CET1 points, 6.2% of market value), but only $23,178M–$33,484M above where the requirement is heading by 2028 (bank-equity-output.md section A). So: modestly over-capitalized against the forward requirement, and closing the gap.

- **If under levered, is it under threat of a hostile acquisition? If over levered, how imminent is the bankruptcy threat?**
  No. Market value $877,084M (`company.equity_value`); no acquirer exists and bank-holding-company rules forbid one in practice. The discipline on excess capital comes from investors' ROTCE expectations and from the pay plans tied to ROTCE.

- **How good are the potential projects that the company can invest in?**
  Good on the evidence: every segment earns well above the 8.27% cost of equity (lesson 14). Balance-sheet growth absorbed $25,787M of CET1 at 11.5% in 2025 (ΔRWA $224,232M; bank-equity-output.md section B) and still left an ROE of 17%.

- **What is the best path that you see for your firm to get from its actual to its optimal debt ratio?**
  Keep doing what it is doing: grow RWA where segment ROE is high and buy back the rest at a pace that brings CET1 down toward ~13% by 2028. At the 1Q26 pace ($8,328M a quarter) the new $50,000M authorization lasts about six quarters (section G).

## Notes

The bank path to 'optimal' is a payout path, so leverage and payout signals merge for JPMorgan.
