# JPMorgan Chase — what the company says, by signal

Verbatim passages from the filings in `sources/filings/`, each with file and line. Nothing here was published after 2026-06-30; the latest passage is from the 8-K filed 2026-06-25.

## Control

S-1. Proxy, Lead Independent Director letter (`sources/filings/jpm-def14a-2026-04-06.md`, line 69): "We are committed to a successful CEO transition at the right time. Importantly, succession is not delegated to one person or one committee."

S-2. Proxy, succession planning (`sources/filings/jpm-def14a-2026-04-06.md`, line 966): "The Board is focused on enabling an orderly CEO transition to take place in the medium-term. As part of succession planning, the Board continues to oversee management's development of several Operating Committee members who are well-known to shareholders as strong potential candidates to succeed Mr. Dimon."

S-3. 8-K item 5.02 (`sources/filings/jpm-8-k-2026-06-25-items-502.md`, lines 59–75): Doug Petno and Troy Rohrbaugh "have been elected Co-Presidents of the Firm, effective immediately"; Marianne Lake "has decided to retire"; the Compensation & Management Development Committee approved "one-time Retention and Continuity equity based awards" of $30 million each to Petno and Rohrbaugh and $20 million each to Mary Erdoes and Jennifer Piepszak, which "100% cliff-vest after three years and are subject to an additional Vesting Performance Condition that requires the Firm to achieve a three-year average return on tangible common equity ("ROTCE") of 12% for calendar years 2026, 2027 and 2028." Purpose: "to preserve top qualified internal succession candidates, maintain key Operating Committee continuity through any leadership transition."

S-4. 8-K item 5.02(e) (`sources/filings/jpm-8-k-2026-01-22-items-502.md`): CEO 2025 pay $43.0 million (2024: $39.0 million), of which $36.5 million in Performance Share Units whose "financial metric (return on tangible common equity ... "ROTCE") with absolute and relative performance goals" is unchanged.

## Lifecycle

S-5. 10-K capital management objectives (`sources/filings/jpm-10-k-2026-02-13.md`, line 3935 onward): "Maintain sufficient capital in order to continue to build and invest in the Firm's businesses through normal economic cycles and in stressed environments; Retain flexibility to take advantage of future investment opportunities; ... Meet capital distribution objectives."

S-6. 10-Q outlook (`sources/filings/jpm-10-q-2026-05-01.md`, lines 357–369): "Management expects net interest income to be approximately $103 billion and net interest income excluding Markets to be approximately $95 billion, market dependent. Management expects adjusted expense to be approximately $105 billion, market dependent. Management expects the net charge-off rate in Card Services to be approximately 3.4%."

## Hurdle and returns

S-7. 10-K line-of-business equity (`sources/filings/jpm-10-k-2026-02-13.md`, line 4256): "Each LOB and Corporate is allocated capital by taking into consideration a variety of factors including capital levels of similarly rated peers and applicable regulatory capital requirements. ROE is measured and internal targets for expected returns are established as key measures of an LOB's performance." No numeric hurdle or return target appears in the 10-K, 10-Q or proxy.

## Leverage (regulatory capital, used in place of the debt-ratio signal)

S-8. 10-K (`sources/filings/jpm-10-k-2026-02-13.md`, line 3913): "A strong capital position is essential to the Firm's business strategy and competitive position. Maintaining a strong balance sheet to manage through economic volatility is a strategic imperative of the Firm's Board of Directors, CEO and Operating Committee. The Firm's "fortress balance sheet" philosophy focuses on risk-adjusted returns, strong capital and robust liquidity."

S-9. 10-K (same file, line 3935 onward): the Firm addresses its objectives by "Establishing internal minimum capital requirements"; "The internal minimum capital levels consider the Firm's regulatory capital requirements as well as an internal assessment of capital adequacy." No numeric internal minimum or CET1 target is disclosed.

S-10. 8-K, 2026 stress test press release (`sources/filings/jpm-8-k-2026-06-24-items-801-901.md`, Exhibit 99): "the Firm's current Stress Capital Buffer ("SCB") requirement of 2.5% will remain unchanged through September 30, 2027 ... As a result, the Firm's current Standardized Common Equity Tier 1 ("CET1") capital ratio requirement including regulatory buffers continues to be 11.5%." Dimon: "Our fortress balance sheet, with significant excess capital and robust liquidity ..."

S-11. 10-Q Key Regulatory Developments (`sources/filings/jpm-10-q-2026-05-01.md`, lines 1656–1662): "Under the rules currently in effect, the Firm's GSIB surcharge, calculated as of December 31, 2025, would be 5.5% with an effective date of January 1, 2028. If the GSIB Surcharge Re-Proposal were to be adopted as proposed, the Firm estimates that the 5.5% GSIB surcharge would be reduced to 5.2%." And: "the changes in requirements reflected in the U.S. Basel III Re-Proposal and the GSIB Surcharge Re-Proposal, taken together, would result in an increase in the Firm's required CET1 capital of approximately 4% as compared to the CET1 capital requirement that, under current rules, would become effective on January 1, 2028." Comments were due June 18, 2026.

S-12. 10-Q (`sources/filings/jpm-10-q-2026-05-01.md`, line 198): "At each of March 31, 2026 and December 31, 2025, the Advanced risk-based ratios were more binding on the Firm than the Standardized risk-based ratios."

## Debt design

S-13. 10-K (`sources/filings/jpm-10-k-2026-02-13.md`, line 977): the risk that regulators require "the issuance of, or prohibiting the redemption of, capital instruments in a manner inconsistent with JPMorganChase's capital management strategy." The 2026 issuance 8-Ks (`jpm-8-k-2026-01-22-items-801-901.md`, `jpm-8-k-2026-02-05-items-801-901.md`, `jpm-8-k-2026-04-23-items-801-901.md`, `jpm-8-k-2026-06-02-items-801-901.md`) are all floating or fixed-to-floating notes due 2030–2037 under the senior or subordinated indenture.

## Payout

S-14. 10-K capital actions (`sources/filings/jpm-10-k-2026-02-13.md`, lines 4280–4303): "The Firm's common stock dividends are planned as part of the Capital Management governance framework in line with the Firm's capital management objectives." Buybacks: "does not establish specific price targets or timetables. Management determines the amount and timing ... based on various factors, including market conditions; ... the Firm's capital position (taking into account goodwill and intangibles); organic capital generation; current and proposed future capital requirements; and other investment opportunities."

S-15. 8-K, 2026 stress test press release (`sources/filings/jpm-8-k-2026-06-24-items-801-901.md`): "its Board of Directors intends to increase the quarterly common stock dividend to $1.65 per share (up from the current $1.50 per share) for the third quarter of 2026" and "has authorized a new common share repurchase program of $50 billion, effective July 1, 2026." Dimon: "The new share repurchase program provides us with the flexibility to deploy capital in ways that enhance shareholder value over time."

S-16. 8-K, 2025 stress test press release (`sources/filings/jpm-8-k-2025-07-01-items-801-901.md`): the 2025 program was also $50 billion, and the dividend went to $1.50 from $1.40, "our second this year, represents a sustainable level of capital distribution."
