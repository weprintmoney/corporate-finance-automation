# JPMorgan Chase & Co. (JPM) — Lesson 01: Valuation: The Big Picture / What is Corporate Finance?

### Lesson 01 — Valuation: The Big Picture / What is Corporate Finance?

Quick mode: only the question-bank entries tagged with a signal. Source lesson: [../../01-foundations-and-discount-rates/lesson-01/lesson-overview.md](../../01-foundations-and-discount-rates/lesson-01/lesson-overview.md).

## Module 1 Step — Questions for This Lesson

- **Which company, and why is it a good subject (data available, decisions to judge)?**
  JPMorgan Chase & Co., the largest US bank holding company (total net revenue $182,447M in 2025, `company.revenue`). It is in the backtest set as the bank: a test of where the course's firm-level tools stop. Data is complete (10-K 2025, 10-Q 1Q26, proxy, every 8-K to 2026-06-25), and the decisions to judge are capital ones: how much CET1 to hold above the requirement, how much to pay out, and how to staff the CEO succession.

- **Where does it sit on the corporate life cycle, judged by growth, margins, reinvestment and cash use?**
  Mature, still growing. Revenue went from $128,695M (2022, facts `revenue-2022`) to $182,447M (2025), helped by the 2023 First Republic acquisition and higher rates; 2025 grew 2.8% and 1Q26 revenue was up 10% on 1Q25 (facts `revenue-2026q1`). Reinvestment for a bank is regulatory capital: risk-weighted assets rose $224,232M in 2025 (bank-equity-output.md section B), and the company still returned 85.7% of net income. That is a mature bank that both grows its balance sheet and returns most of what it earns.

## Notes

The life-cycle row in read.md: mature, not declining; reinvestment is measured as growth in risk-weighted assets, not capex.
