## Cumulative Project — Questions for This Lesson

- **Under, over or correctly levered?**

  Under-levered at market values: 14.41% against an optimum of 34% (APV 34%), and below the peer median debt ratio 23.7% and the Building Supply industry 18.9% (`facts: peer-median-debt-ratio`, `industry-debt-ratio-building-supply`). At book values it looks heavily levered (82%), which is the frame management and the agencies use: book debt with leases to EBITDA was 2.61x at fiscal 2025 year-end and 2.53x on May 3 debt (2.37x with notes at market, not comparable) (`facts: debt-to-ebitda-*`). The stated plan is to reduce debt, away from the optimum.

- **Under threat of hostile acquisition?**

  No. At $351.7B of equity no bidder or activist can lever HD; the cost of being under-levered is about $2.5–16B (section 6 and 9 measures), under 5% of value.

- **How good are the potential projects?**

  Organic projects (stores, supply chain) are few: about 15 new stores in fiscal 2026 and capex at 2.5% of sales (`facts: stores-guided-new-fy2026`). The projects HD is choosing, distribution acquisitions, earn low accounting returns (2.5% operating margin for SRS/GMS after amortization, 5.6% before) and are priced with goodwill. Return on the whole business (20.41%) still beats the hurdle; return on the new capital likely does not yet.

- **Best path from actual to optimal?**

  If HD wanted the optimum, it would resume buybacks funded by new long-term fixed-rate notes. It is doing the opposite for now. The realistic path is: finish paying down commercial paper, then resume buybacks with cash flow from fiscal 2027, holding debt flat in dollars so the ratio drifts with value. The financing gain is small (section 6: $2.49 a share on DCF).
