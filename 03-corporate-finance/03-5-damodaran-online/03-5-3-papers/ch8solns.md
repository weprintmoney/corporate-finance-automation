## **Chapter 8**

**In many of the solutions, where there is a change in the cost of capital and you are called upon to compute the change in firm value, there is insufficient information to estimate future growth in savings. You have two choices: (1) assume a reasonable perpetual growth rate or (2) a growth rate of zero. While the latter will yield more conservative estimates of value change, I have used it in all of the solutions.**

It is true that the return on equity can be increased by borrowing money, since the aftertax cost of debt is likely to be lower than the return on assets (which is currently equal to the return on equity) of 12.75%. Borrowing money will also increase the cost of equity, however. The net effect will determine whether leverage will increase firm value. If the

business risk of the firm is high (a high unlevered beta), then the increase in the cost of equity may exceed the increase in return on equity.
