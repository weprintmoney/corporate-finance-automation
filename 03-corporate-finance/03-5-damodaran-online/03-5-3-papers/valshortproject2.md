# Company & Business Overview

- DISH Network Corporation is a provider of satellite television, 5G wireless broadband (pre and post-paid), and streaming services in the United States. Founded in 1980, DISH has been a pioneer in the television industry, consistently evolving and innovating to meet the changing needs of consumers.
- DISH's services include satellite television, streaming video, and broadband through its multiple brands: DISH TV, Sling TV, and Boost Mobile. DISH TV offers a wide range of channels and packages, including local and international channels, premium movie channels, and sports networks. Sling TV, a leading over-the-top (OTT) internet television service, provides customers with a flexible and cost-effective way to watch live and on-demand television programming. Boost Mobile, acquired in 2020, offers prepaid mobile services, marking DISH's significant move into the wireless space.
- DISH was previously known for its advanced technology, including its award-winning DVR technology, the Hopper, which allows customers to record, pause, and rewind live TV. The company is also recognized for its commitment to excellent customer service and its dedication to providing affordable, reliable, and innovative solutions to its customers.
- As it moves forward, DISH is focused on navigating the evolving media landscape, investing in 5G network, and expanding its presence in the wireless market, while continuing to deliver entertainment and communication services to its 17M customers.

### **DISH Network | 2022 Simplified Results**

DISH Network is endeavoring to enter the wireless carrier business via its existing spectrum holdings valued in excess of \$30B and divested assets from the T-Mobile + Sprint Merger. As part of this settlement, DISH acquired 7.98 million subscribers from the Boost brand pre-paid cellular business, received roaming and connectivity agreements with T-Mobile, and have an option in 2023 to acquire another \$3.59B in spectrum to further bolster their network coverage and capacity.

However, Dish Network's growth prospects are uncertain and far from guaranteed, as the company faces intense competition in the TV streaming industry and the recent trend of cord-cutting has impacted the company's traditional satellite TV business. Additionally, DISH Network faces regulatory and legal risks related to the rollout of its 5G network, which may impact the company's financial performance in the future.

# An uncertain future erodes value….

#### **Tailwinds moving their business:**

- As a result of the T-Mobile + Sprint merger, they got access to significantly discounted divested assets, giving them a head start on a nationwide 5G wireless buildout, a customer base to serve, and the ability to be a carrier and sell services day one as an MVNO.
- DISH has the opportunity to provide a truly bundled content experience to a multi-modal customer base comprised of nearly 18M customers today. This bundle positions them in a similar position to their largest competitor, AT&T (including DirectTV).
- DISH has the opportunity to disrupt the wireless competitor set with their unique network architecture affords them the opportunity to provide differentiated services, especially to enterprise customers looking to take advantage of their 5G network for specialized applications in infrastructure, mining, manufacturing, utilities, etc. These sectors will drive much higher revenue opportunities and will have far less churn than retail customers.
- DISH is unencumbered with legacy wireless systems, infrastructure, or approaches that historically has driven inefficiency in operations and resulted in lower operating income for their competition. This lower cost basis will also allow them to charge more competitive rates to attract new customers to their network in the next few years.
- DISH is materially on track to achieve the 2023 70% FCC build out requirements, allowing them to serve 70% of the country with this service, this summer. They have 70 million pops served today (22% of the country), 50 major metropolitan markets, and 5 handsets that support their band available for Boost Infinite. As a result of hitting this buildout requirement, CapEx should ramp down substantially.
- For their size and number of customers, DISH is relatively spectrum rich a key ingredient in building out nationwide coverage and the wireless capacity required by consumers. They could liquidate some of their (\$30B book value | Est. \$50B market value) holdings to support funding their build out, if required. They can also continue to collateralize their debt with this asset.

#### **Headwinds facing their business are significant:**

- Large subscriber churn far in excess the industry average resulting in year over year revenue decline in traditional satellite TV business which is funding (subsidizing) the 5G build out
- Increased TV carriage fees with contentious negotiations and disputes are driving fixed costs higher and must be spread over a smaller customer base to provide the same service levels.
- Downgraded debt ratings (S&P CCC-) have resulted in last debt issuance at 11.75% and a \$24B debt balance weighs on the company's cash flows (with an additional \$11B expected in the next 18 months to support the 2025 5G build out requirements). In their recent earnings call, their Chairman stated, "our debt is trading at 20% yield to maturity, so the debt market is in essence closed for us right now."
- Pending 5G buildout targets (FCC regulated) pose government claw back of strategic assets if agreed settlement dates are missing.
- Their ability to create and ultimately have customers pay for a differentiated 5G network offering is unproven all in a completive oligopoly market with 3 incumbent participants holding circa 99% of the market share today. As of April 2023, very few customers have adopted their network and almost no Boost customers have moved to their network.
- DISH lost access to a key spectrum band due to improper designated bidding entities. They will have to re-acquire this spectrum as early as Q4 2023 and it will be critical to their wireless network build out.
- DISH experienced a significant cyber breach in Q1 2023, resulting in 4x the industry average customer churn (reduced gross adds) and increased cost of service by \$30M, as customers were unable to sign up for service, log in to accounts, pay bills, and other basic services for over a month.
- DISH faces both inbound and outbound litigation threats, including hundreds of millions of dollars in risk for breach of intellectual property rights of other tech companies.

**A two stage free cash flow to the firm discount cash flow valuation model was used to value DISH network. Year 1-3 is both high growth and high re-investment and over the long-time horizon gradually decrease to a blend of the industries that they are in (Pay TV and Wireless).**

**Below is a narrative of DISH Networks key growth metrics which had an impact on the final underlying valuation.** 

- **Revenues:** As the traditional DISH broadcast business revenues continue to decline from customer churn, their future growth will come from their nationwide 5G network buildout. DISH is endeavoring to be a low-cost provider to consumers and focus on enterprise clients as a key customer target. Their cloud native, fully open RAN 5G platform architecture provides the only growth engine for DISH, with both retail and enterprise 5G applications revenue driving growth over the next 10 years.
  - Below average Average Revenue Per Unit (ARPU) and customer capture were closely considered in the development of the revenue model, with Year 10 revenue equaling \$29.83B (approx. 80% or \$23.86B from Wireless)
  - According to IBISWorld, the Total Addressable Market (TAM) for wireless telecommunication carriers was \$322.8B in 2022. Assuming linear 10 year growth rate of 0.5% for a conservative proxy for TAM growth, DISH would need to acquire 7% market share over that period.
  - According to Verizon, there is an additional \$7-8B TAM for private / enterprise wireless, which has not yet been captures. DISH is positioning their offer in this segment and will likely be a catalyst for much of their early market capture.
- **Operating Margins:** The model assumes that DISH's 5G network gets completed in 2025 (on time) and customer adoption and margins grow to a representative blend of industry averages between broadcast and cellular. Achieving industry margins will be difficult for DISH and rests on their ability to create a differentiated value proposition for their customers. Given their technology architecture choices, the lack of legacy networks, and an OpEx focused lease model, it is reasonable to expect operating margins to approach their peers, even with a lower ARPU.
- **Reinvestment:** DISH has previously made significant capital investments in spectrum and infrastructure over the last four years and must continue to re-invest heavily in business to complete their 5G network buildout in 2023, 2024 and 2025. These needs will dramatically drop off, allowing them to generate growth from their large asset investments while pulling back on their reinvestment rate. The model includes \$16.9B in total CapEx investment, which is inclusive of the \$11B estimates over the next 18 months.

# Discounted Cash Flow Valuation Narrative

A little bit venture, going concern, and distress.

- **Sales/Capital**: As a result of start-up like revenue growth from 5G and significant CapEx, their Sales/Capital ratio remains the same through 2025, before escalating quickly thereafter. After that, this previous CapEx should fuel revenues and reinvestment should exceed industry averages for 3-5 years before returning to a blend of the industry averages between broadcast and cellular.
- **Return on Capital:** DISH does not yet have a demonstrated competitive advantage and have pivoted from one shrinking business to another highly competitive one with price sensitive customers and potentially without a differentiated offer. Their ability to attract and retain credit worthy postpaid customers (with higher ARPU) and convert prepaid customers is critical in the generation of higher Wireless revenues and bringing down EBIT via network utilization, ultimately driving higher ROIC.
- **Cost of Capital:** Cost of capital was estimated at 12.16% using the US multi-business industry average and actual debt rating (B3/B- equivalent) approach. The cost of capital will decline as new 5G business generates cash to pay down debt and the stable growth period is entered. The wireless business will also become a greater percentage of their overall business and therefore ultimately approaches the wireless telecom industry average for their US only peer group. A risk-free rate of 3.46% was used based on the May 10, 2023 US 10-Year T-Bond rate. The assumption was made that this rate will not prevail in perpetuity, and the long-run average of 2.75% after Year 10 is more appropriate.
- **Default Risk:** Given DISH CCC- rating (B3/B- equivalent) from S&P, the 10-year probability of failure is estimated at 50.38%. This was estimated based on their actual corporate bond rating. Their Chairman recently stated in an earnings call that they have a "narrow window" to address their capital structure and needs, including the \$14.7B of distressed debt (representing 60.7% of total debt outstanding). A book value of assets approach was used to calculate the proceeds if DISH fails. Given their substantially valuable spectrum holdings (\$30B BV) and other capital assets, it is assumed that proceeds will net 75% of BV of assets.

# Relative Valuation Methodology

- **Why EBITDA?** EBITDA is an appropriate metric to assess the financial performance of both cable, satellite and telecom companies which DISH primarily operates in.
  - EBITDA is a measure of comparing a telecom company's operational effectiveness and financial stability. EBITDA is less impacted by accounting considerations and excludes non-cash expenses such as depreciation and amortization. This makes it a useful metric for comparing the profitability of different telecommunications companies, as they may have different levels of debt (financing structure) and capital expenditures (associated depreciation).
  - Additionally, depreciation is a significant non-cash expense for capital intensive businesses. Given that cable, satellite and telecom companies typically pour a lot of money into network infrastructure, EBITDA offers a more precise way to compare them. By excluding the effects of accounting and financing decisions tied to capital investments, EBITDA provides a clearer comparison for operating profitability among similar companies. For example, is particularly true when one company is heavily involved in substantial capital projects while another is not, or similar companies could use different depreciation methods or useful lives, which skews the comparison. Fundamentally, it allows investors to concentrate on the company's core profitability without considering the accounting considerations of their capital costs in their evaluation.
  - There are limitations of using this approach, as it would show two capital intensive businesses deploying different CapEx to achieve the same EBITDA inappropriate lower (or higher) than the other. Moreover, it further breakdowns working across multiple disparate industries.

### • **General Approach**

- Data was collected from S&P Capital IQ and 20 United States companies in the appropriate sectors/industries were used to compare to DISH.
- A correlation matrix was created to identify the independent variable that is most correlated with the dependent variable, while also attempting to reduce the risk of multicollinearity between the independent variables. The correlation matrix is a table that shows the correlation coefficients between all pairs of variables in a dataset. The correlation coefficient is a measure of the linear relationship between two variables. A correlation coefficient of 1 indicates a perfect positive correlation, a correlation coefficient of -1 indicates a perfect negative correlation, and a correlation coefficient of 0 indicates no correlation.
- Forward looking EV/EBITDA is used since the Enterprise Value (EV) in the numerator reflects future expected value and aligns with DISH growth trajectory goals in new sectors.
