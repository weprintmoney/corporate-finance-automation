## **Session 18C: Post class test solutions**

- 1. **a. Young companies with long life R&D.** For a company's return on capital to change a lot, its operating income has to change dramatically more, on a proportional basis, than its invested capital. The operating income change is the difference between the current year's R&D and the amortization of prior year's R&D. That change is likely to be highest for young firms with growing R&D. Since these firms have not been around for long, the change in invested capital is also likely to be small (since you are capitalizing R&D for the company's life).
