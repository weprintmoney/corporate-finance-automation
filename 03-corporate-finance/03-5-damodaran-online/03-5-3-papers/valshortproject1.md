### CHIPOTLE MEXICAN GRILL

![](_page_1_Picture_1.jpeg)

➢ Chipotle Mexican Grill Inc operates as a fast-casual restaurant chain that specializes in Mexican cuisine. It has stores located in the United States of America, Canada, France, United Kingdom and Germany. ➢ Chipotle started its operations 1993 in Denver Colorado, with a single store and has grown to become the second largest fast food chain in the world by market capitalization. Chipotle operates 3134 stores in the USA, 32 stores in Canada, 13 in the UK, 6 in France and 2 in Germany. ➢ Some of the competitive advantages that Chipotle have: ✓ Fresh and High-Quality ingredients ✓ Fast casual dining experience ✓Customizable dishes

These are analyzed in depth on the discounted cash flow valuation.

➢ The purpose of this report is to value the equity shares in Chipotle. The results are summarized below:

#### Narrative

![](_page_2_Picture_2.jpeg)

➢ The valuation of Chipotle was conducted using a two-stage free cash flow to firm model. This model considers an initial period of high growth from year 1 to year 5, which gradually decreases to the risk-free rate of the United States of America. In this context, the competitive advantages of Chipotle are discussed below as they play a crucial role in determining some of the assumptions made for the valuation. ➢ Chipotle, due to its strong brand name, can charge a premium for its product and with the company is able to enjoy higher margins. Some of the competitive advantages that chipotle has that created this strong brand name are listed below: ✓ Fresh and High-Quality ingredients: Chipotle is very select with its suppliers, and they must meet rigorous standards such as avoiding the use of antibiotics or added hormones when raising the animals. Chipotle also prepares smaller batches of food throughout the day so they can always be fresh. ✓ Fast casual dining experience: Chipotle was the pioneer of fast casual dining, and this new style of restaurant was loved by the public as they can enjoy a high quality and nutritional meal at a speed never seen before, customers are able to enjoy a fast and easy meal without compromising on quality. ✓ Customizable dishes: Customers are able to tailor any mix of ingredients that they want in their burrito or bowl, this is a tremendous advantage as customers can order a meal that fits their individual taste and needs. It also creates a more engaging and interactive dining experience as customers feel like they have a greater level of control over their dining experience. This also helps to build loyalty as it will be harder for clients to find other restaurants that have the bowl or burrito they created on their menu.

#### Narrative

![](_page_3_Picture_2.jpeg)

➢ Below are some of the key assumptions regarding how the competitive advantages will be able to continue or improve through time: ✓ Operating margin: Chipotles pre-tax operating margin has continued to gradually revert to the levels of margin seen prior to the food poisoning issue\* that the company faced in December of 2015. Margin averaged around 17.13% from the beginning of 2012 to the end of 2015, while the company still had not implemented chipotlanes (drive through lanes) which contribute to margins as sales coming from chipotlanes require smaller work force and real state. ✓ Revenue growth: Chipotle number of units will increase from 3,187 to 5,887 on year 10 averaging a total opening of 270 stores per year, opening new units at this pace will enable to chipotle to expand without seeing a decline in the quality of its service. It has also been proven that Chipotle is able to repass inflation to its customers, so it will also be a driver of its revenue growth. The revenue growth will gradually move towards the risk free rate as the firm enters a stable growth phase after year 10. ✓ Cost of Capital: Chipotle operates in an inelastic sector and has very little debt which lead the companies cost of capital to be low. The current cost capital was maintained at perpetuity. ✓ Return on Capital: Chipotle´s return on capital also continues to revert to the levels seen prior to the food poising issue which averaged 24.54%. So, it is expected that the return on capital will return to this level. ➢ The return on capital and the cost of capital were able to be maintained at perpetuity as in the fast food/ dining sector when a company is able to create a loyal customer base due to a good product it is very hard for it to be replaced as the company benefits from its size by gaining more leverage when negotiating with its suppliers and is also able to scale its marketing. Another contributing factor is that there is not much innovation that can take place with food, it is improbable that a new product is created and disrupts Chipotles existing one. Analyzing the sector, it is also able to notice that segment leaders tend to enjoy the competitive advantages for decades.

*\*Since the incident Chipotle has implemented an enhanced food safety program, which includes DNA-testing ingredients before they are shipped to restaurants, changes to food prep and handling practices, and is offering paid sick leave to disincentivize sick employees from working.*

# CONCLUSION

![](_page_7_Picture_1.jpeg)

➢ From the discounted cash flow approach, it was concluded that Chipotle is trading below its fair value. Nonetheless, it was shown to be currently traded above its implied price by both regressions. It can be concluded the Chipotle stock is undervalued whilst also being overpriced. ➢ The discounted cash flow value of Chipotle is \$2569.19 while the market price is currently at \$2046.51. Given this information, it is recommended to buy shares of Chipotle. Taking in consideration the current market price it appears that the market does not believe that the company will continue to be able to expand its margins. ➢ The pricing, on the other hand, suggests that the company is overpriced and should be trading at \$1588.82. ➢ The final recommendation would be to **BUY** shares of Chipotle if you have a long-term investment horizon, as the discounted cash flow valuation involves a detailed fundamental analysis of the company, discarding short-term price fluctuations.
