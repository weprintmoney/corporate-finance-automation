# Lesson 14 — Earnings and Cash Flows

## Overview

Some of the most-widely used return measures for projects and companies are, not surprisingly, built around accounting earnings and measurement of investment (book value). That said, they are flawed and can bias you away from good projects and towards bad projects, if used blindly. By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

**Learning objectives:**
- Measure an accounting return to equity investors, as well as to all capital providers in a firm, for either an individual project or for the entire firm.

## Project Questions

- What return on capital did your firm generate last year?
- If you have returns on capital over time for your firm, is there a trend in those returns?
- How do the returns on capital (equity) compare to the costs of capital (equity)?
- Is there a typical project for this firm? If yes, what does it look like in terms of life (long term or short term), investment needs and cash flow patterns?
- Are the projects in the future likely to look like the projects in the past? Why or why not?
