# Final Project — Professor's Checklist Letter

Professor Damodaran's letter to the class with a to-do list for the final project, copied word for word. Added 2026-11-03. Only the layout is changed: section names are bold and sub-points are bullets.

---

I know that the project is not due for a few weeks, but since we are approaching Thanksgiving and Christmas, and time speeds up, it tends to be put on the back burner for too long. To help you get started, if you have not, or to keep moving, if you have, I thought I would get this list of things to do early. So, with no further ado, here you go:

**Corporate Governance:** Assess your company's corporate governance by looking at how much power shareholders have in the company to change the course of management. In making this assessment, you may want to look at:

- The top management of the company, their tenure at the company and shareholdings.
- The board of directors composition, tenure and activism
- Voting rights on shares and corporate charter modifications that affect shareholder power
- Shareholding breakdown, into individual, institutional and insider, and what it tells you about corporate governance

**Risk and Hurdle Rates:** While the end game in this section is coming up with a cost of capital for your firm, recognize that I am less interested in the mechanics and more interested in your logic. In getting to a hurdle rate, you will have to:

- Return regression and what you can learn about a company's risk profile and past stock price performance
- Currency choice and how it plays out in a riskfree rate
- Business mix and leverage, and the resulting effects on beta
- Geographical mix of operations and equity risk premium
- Default risk and its effect on the cost of borrowing
- Mix of debt and equity, and the cost of capital for the company and its businesses

**Past and future investments:** It is a good idea to take a look at the company's past investments and whether they were good or bad investments. In making this assessment you should consider:

- What a typical project for the company looks like...
- Accounting returns (ROIC and ROE) earned by the company and how they compare to costs of capital and equity
- An assessment of why the company is generating the returns on projects that it is (in terms of competitive advantages and strengths or weaknesses
- An extrapolation into the future where you examine whether the company can continue to earn excess returns, if it is making more than the cost of capital, or start earning positive excess returns, if it is making less than the cost of capital.

**Optimal capital structure:** You need to compute the optimal debt ratio for your company

- Estimate the cost of capital at different debt ratios. You are welcome to use my spreadsheet (http://www.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pc/capstru.xlsx) to make this assessment.
- Assess how your firm's debt ratio compares to the sector. You can just compare the debt ratio for your firm to the average for the sector. If you feel up to it, you can try running a regression of debt ratios of firms in your sector against the fundamentals that drive debt ratio (Look at the entertainment sector regression I ran for Disney in the notes).

**Debt design:** As you work your way through or towards the debt design part, here are a few sundry thoughts to take away for the analysis:

- The heart of debt design should be the intuitive analysis, where you look at what a typical project/investment is for your firm (perhaps in each business it is in) and design the most flexible debt you can, given the risk exposure.
- The quantitative tools (the regression of firm value/ operating income versus macro variables, using http://www.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pc/macrodur.xlsx) may or may not yield useful data. The bottom-up approach (using sector averages) offer more promise. If you have a non-US company, a US company with little history or get strange results, stick with just the intuitive approach.
- Compare the actual debt to your perfect debt (either from the intuitive approach or from the quantitative approach) and make a judgment on what your company should do.

**Dividend analysis:** We developed a framework for analyzing whether your company pays out too much or too little in dividends in class yesterday. You can read ahead to chapter 11, if you want, and use the spreadsheet at the link http://www.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pc/dividends.xlsx to do your analysis.

- Examine whether your company has returned cash to its stockholders over the last few years (5-10 or whatever time your firm has been in existence) and if yes, in what form (dividends or stock buybacks). The information should be in your statement of cash flows.
- Make a judgment on whether your company should return more or less cash to its stockholders.

**Valuation:** This is a corporate finance class, with valuation at the tail end. Since we will not have done much on valuation, this section is optional but it provides a capstone to your project but I promise not to look to deeply into it. Knowing how nervous some of you are about doing a valuation, I have a spreadsheet to ease the valuation: http://www.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pc/fcffsimpleginzu.xlsx You will notice that the spreadsheet has some default assumptions built in (to prevent you from creating inconsistent assumptions). I let you change the defaults and feel free to do so, if you feel comfortable with the valuation process. If not, my suggestion is that you leave the inputs alone. If you find yourself stuck or just want to watch a bad homemade movie, try this YouTube guide that I have to using the spreadsheet: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8pfDYA_UEGI

**Project write-up and formatting:** If you are thinking of the write-up for the project and formatting choices, you can look at some past group reports on the webpage for the class. (It should be in the description of the final project). I prefer brevity and I want to emphasize the page limit of 7-9 pages on the report. As a general rule, steer away from explaining mechanics - how you unlevered or levered betas -and spend more time analyzing your output (why should your company have a high beta? And what do you make of their really high or low return on capital?). If you want to see examples of past projects, try this link: https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pdfiles/cfshortprojs/

Hope you don't find this list too intimidating, but having a checklist can get you moving. Good luck!
