1. Stockholder Analysis: Who has the power and what are the potential conflicts of
   interest?
 Background:
 Skanska is one of the world's leading project development and construction groups, concentrated on
 selected home markets in the Nordic region, other European countries and in the U.S. Skanska was
 established in Sweden in 1887 and started manufacturing concrete products. It quickly diversified
 into a construction company and within 10 years the company received its first international order.
 The company played an important role in building Sweden’s infrastructure including its roads, power
 plants, offices and housing.
 Since 1950’s, Skanska expanded to international markets, notably South America. In 1971, Skanska
 entered the US market which constitutes a good opportunity for the company where around 30% of
 its activity is concentrated compared to 25% in Sweden. The Company was made public in 1965 and
 was listed in Stockholm Stock Exchange.
 In 2013, Skanska counted more than 57,000 employees with a market capitalization of 54 bn SEK.
 Investors:
 Main investors in Skanska are mainly:
   Pension fund “ALECTA” which owns 7.35% of the shares.
   Industrivarden AB which owns 4.84% of the shares. Industrivarden is an active investor where
     active ownership role is exercised through board representation.
   NORDEA BANK owns 4.24% of the shares. Nordea Bank is as well an activist investor.
   SWEDBANK ROBUR mutual fund Company that owns 3.29%.
   SEB Bank
   Lundbergs which owns 2.64% is an investment company that manages and develops a number
     of companies by being an active, long term owner.
   Skanska which owns 2.27% of its own companies
   AMF FORSAKRING which owns 2.18% is a pension fund.
   SHB FUNDS which owns 2.06% mainly a short term investor.
   The remaining of the top 17 investors are mainly banks and institutions.

 There are a number of investors which is a mix between pension funds, mutual funds that are
 considered as passive investors, and their reaction to bad management of the firm is to sell their
 shares and invest somewhere else. However there are a certain number of activist investors that
 usually seek to change the way companies are run. The presence of these investors should therefore
 increase the power of all stockholders relative to managers. In this context, the managers can be
 accountable and can be removed if they make bad decisions.
 Among the top 17 investors, there is a considerable number that are Swedish or from Nordic
 countries
 2 series of shares for Skanska: Series A shares and series B shares.
 Each Series A share carries 10 votes and each Series B share carries one vote. Series B shares are

2. Risk and Return: How risky is your firm and what are its hurdle rates?
 Risk Free Rate:

 The risk‐free rate we should use for our analysis of the Swedish Company Skanska should be in the
 same currency that Skanska’s cash flow is estimated in. The cash‐flow of Skanska is in Swedish Krona,
 the risk‐free rate to be used is the Swedish Krona’s risk‐free rate.
         Risk Free Rate = Local currency Government bond rate – Government Default Spread
 The government bond rate has a default spread component in it in addition to the risk free rate. The
 Moody’s rating for Sweden sovereign debt is Aaa. The Swedish government default spread is 0.00%;
 the risk‐free rate is therefore the same as the government bond rate which is 0.9%.
 The Swedish government is perceived to have no default risk; the risk‐free rate is equal to the
 government bond rate. The risk‐free rate used for our analysis of Skanska is 0.9%

 The conventional practice on ERP is to estimate an ERP based upon where the company is
