L'Oréal manufactures, markets and distributes beauty products to consumers and professionals.
Founded in France in 1909, L'Oréal is present in 130 countries in all 5 continents and employs
over 78,000 persons worldwide. L'Oréal SA, a French company, acts a holding company of the
L'Oréal group, composed of 190 fully or jointly owned subsidiaries and 32 brands.

L'Oréal has been run by Jean-Paul Agon as CEO since 2006 and as Chairman and CEO since
2011. As of February 18th, 2016 L'Oréal's market capitalization was €86,559 million with 559
million shares outstanding at a value of €154.65 each.

1.     STOCKHOLDER ANALYSIS AND CORPORATE GOVERNANCE

1.1    Stockholder analysis

The majority of L'Oréal's share is held by two shareholders – the founding family and Nestlé, a
long-standing shareholder and friend of the founding family. These two shareholders (and shares
owned by L'Oréal) total 57.47% of the share capital of L'Oréal, leaving a free float of 42.53%
available to other holders. In more details, the shares are held as follows:

-     The largest shareholder of L'Oréal is the founding family, the Bettencourt Meyers family
      with 33% of the share capital, just below the 33.33% threshold that would require the
      Bettencourt Meyers family to launch a mandatory public offer over the share capital of
      L'Oréal.

-     The second largest shareholder is Nestlé, with 23.08% of the share capital. Nestlé has been
      a shareholder of L'Oréal since 1974; Nestlé and the Bettencourt Meyers family are
      considered as acting "in concert" and are party to a shareholders' agreement that will remain
      in place until at least 6 month after the death of Mrs Liliane Bettencourt. Although the lock-
      up and pre-emption clauses in the shareholders' agreement have lapsed, of this shareholder
      agreement should guarantee a certain stability in the shareholding for the time being.

-     L'Oréal holds 1.24% of its shares.

The other 19 largest investors are institutional investors, such as Norges Bank, Blackrock or the
Vanguard group. As of December 31th 2014, international and French institutional investors held
36.49% of the shares. The marginal investor in L'Oréal can therefore be considered as a well-
diversified institutional investor.

The board is composed of the 15 members set out in Schedule 1. The board seems well balanced
between the Chairman, board members appointed by the Bettencourt Meyers family (3) and by
Nestlé (2) (bringing a total of five board members appointed by the two controlling
shareholders), independent board members (7) and employees' representative (2). The two vice-

              c. Cost of capital

L'Oréal does not detail the maturity of its debt year by year, it only distinguishes its maturity as

3.       INVESTMENTS

L'Oréal indicates that it realizes (a) scientific investments and investments in equipment, mainly
for R&D and innovations, which will likely be long-term6 and (b) marketing investments, made
on an on-going basis, which will likely be short terms.

In 2014, L'Oréal invested €1,008 million as follows: (a) 33% for production and physical
distributions, (b) 42% as marketing investments and (c) the remainder for research and head
offices.

I measured L'Oréal's return on capital as a firm, by taking into account the operational profit
(EBIT) of €3,583.4 million and the book value of capital invested of €20,322.7 million (estimated
as the book value of debt and equity less cash and cash equivalent as of the beginning of the
reference year). This results in an after tax return on capital of 11.76%.

Relative to cost of capital of 4.94%, L'Oréal earns excess returns of 6.82%, with a positive EVA
of €1,385.40 million.

L'Oréal has a lower ROC that the general retail industry average (12.56%), however due to its
lower cost of capital that the industry, it is able to maintain a higher excess return that the

financings, depending on the hedging costs.

Regarding short term debt, L'Oréal seems to have sufficient available funds under its €3,300
committed facilities, however L'Oréal could borrow additional sums to achieve a 30% optimal
D/E ratio.

changes to the reinvestment percentage, L'Oreal valuation would increase, making L'Oréal's
motto true!

L'Oréal follows the recommendations set by the French corporate governance professional body
(AFEP-MEDEF) to determine if a director is an "independent director". The criteria set out by
AFEP-MEDEF to assess independence include that the director may not be an officer or
employee of the company, nor a customer, supplier, investment banker or financial banker of the
company, nor have any family link, and that the director may not be a director of that company
for more than 12 years.
