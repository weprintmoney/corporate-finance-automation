# Lesson 1 — Introduction to the Course

Hello everyone I am so excited to get started with our financial accounting course. Now our goal is to teach you the rules and the mechanics of accrual-based accounting. Not so that you can become accountants or bookkeepers. Instead you will become knowledgeable financial statement users. So you can assess the performance of companies understand how the accounting rules can be subjective and learn when rules can even be manipulated. Now todays lesson will be an introduction of a few basics and then we can start to go deeper and apply our knowledge to actual companies. Alright Many of you have probably heard accounting is the language of business.

In order to evaluate a company's performance we must understand their accounting. It's like learning the alphabet before you learn to read. So accompany has economic events they're hiring and firing and buying and selling distributing manufacturing. They're doing their thing...could be thousands of transactions a day. For each event they must analyze quantify and record the activity. Periodically all of this activity is summarized in the form of or financial statements that tell a story and indicate the economic health of a business. Ultimately the goal is to provide an accurate portrayal of a company so you can make decisions and assessments and not to be dramatic but there is a lot at stake with accounting.

If it's not done properly companies go out of business employees lose their jobs investors and lenders lose their assets. So who uses accounting information for a company investors who want to value a company use accounting data Managers they want to know if a new product will be profitable. Potential employees who are considering going to work for a company. Legislators want to know how a proposed law will affect budgets. Creditors want to know if they should extend credit how much for how long in other words decision-makers accounting is meaningful in almost all business contexts and for many different constituents.

Now we are going to be studying financial accounting which focuses on external users or external decision makers people who don't work at the company. There's also Managerial Accounting that focuses on internal users. The managers running the business Managerial accounting is focused on the detailed results of specific products profitability in different geographies cost of manufacturing et cetera. For financial accounting we have a set of rules that public companies must follow. The financial statements are prepared based on those rules and the financial statements are reporting what has already happened in the past. So let's review the key players in providing these statements Management.

The people running the business record all of the activity and they prepare the financial statements for a large company. This could be a team of accountants led by the chief financial officer and the controller. So the One question that comes to mind how do you know that they're reporting accurate results how can you trust them obviously they want everyone to think they're successful so they can attract more investors. Executives often have bonuses that are contingent on the company's results. That's where the role of the auditor comes into play. The auditor is the independent outside accountant that reviews the company's books and a test that the financial statements are prepared in accordance with the accounting rules.

All public companies are required to undergo an annual audit that is published with their financial data. And since Sarbanes-Oxley legislation or Sox passed in 2002 in the US auditors don't just validate the numbers being reported. They also publish a report on the internal controls of accompany the policies and procedures of how events are recorded. It's an opinion on the overall effectiveness of the accounting process. Think of it as a doctor providing a medical report after a check-up. But in this case they're reporting on the economic health of a business So the companies prepare the financial statements the auditor's validate them then they must file with the regulators in the US company submit their annual financial information called a ten k to the Securities and Exchange Commission.

The SEC. This is the regulatory government agency that oversees the US securities market. Companies also submit quarterly reports called Ten cues and other countries have similar requirements. Now we don't need to go into a lot of detail of the regulatory structure but you can access all of this publicly filed information at the website of the SEC and there are also many other sources of financial information for public companies. This is also a good time to mention that there are different accounting rules in the world. The two most widely followed our US generally accepted accounting principles or gap and International Financial Reporting Standards or IFRS.

Now there's a history behind why we have two sets of rules but just understand that they are very very similar for most of the topics that we covered. However the few differences are a bit contentious. We've had some promises and a bit of drama for many years that the two will converge into one set of rules May maybe not be surprise that some of the differences are political. For example we have an inventory valuation method in the US that's not allowed anywhere else in the world. More importantly we want to learn the rules so that you can analyze the impact.

For example with some areas of accounting we can precisely quantify or value a transaction but in other areas the measurement is more subjective. We also have areas where managers will have choices on how they report. So when they get to choose one inventory valuation method versus another this choice will have different impact on their bottom line Having choices with different consequences and brings me to my next point. There are ethical considerations in accounting. People often think accounting is just strict rules black or white. However managers have discretion and they can sometimes use their discretion opportunistically.
