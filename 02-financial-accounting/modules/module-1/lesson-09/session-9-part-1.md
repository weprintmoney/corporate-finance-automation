# Lesson 9 — Accounting for Taxes

Let's continue our journey through the financial reports and discuss another common line item for most companies, deferred taxes. This is another category that can be seen in both the asset and liability category of the balance sheet. Now, as we've discussed, public companies are required to use accrual-based accounting to report their results to outside investors. This is called financial reporting. And we've discussed many companies follow either U.S. GAAP or International Financial Reporting Standards or rules from other countries. However, there are different accounting rules for tax reporting. Now, you may wonder why there would be different accounting rules.

Well, it does make sense because the goals for a financial reporting are to accurately present the performance of a company to outsiders. Whereas, the goals for tax reporting are for a government to be able to calculate taxes on a company's earnings. The difference in the rules, the financial reporting rules versus the tax reporting rules, creates a difference between the tax expense reported to investors on the income statement versus the taxes actually paid to the governments.
