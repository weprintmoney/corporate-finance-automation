# Lesson 4 — Statement of Stockholders' Equity

All right so let's take a look at our next required financial statement the statement of stockholders equity. Going back to our balance sheet equation, do you recall the two components of stockholders equity? Amounts contributed by investors plus earnings that are retained by the business. The retained earnings are the earnings or net income minus any distributions they make to investors. Now note that these distributions to investors dividends are not considered expenses, they're optional. Whereas expenses are the costs you incur to run your business. Let's return to the Wholefoods balance sheet. You'll notice that for the fiscal year ended 2015 the ending balance for stockholders equity was 3 billion 769 and the next year it decreased to 3 billion 224.

What happened? If you're like me you're nosy and you want more information. What happened during the year that caused the change? Look no further statement of stockholders equity. It gives us a detailed breakdown of the last three years. Let's focus on the most recent year and see what happened and keep in mind what makes this side of the equation go up and what makes it go down. We'll start with last year's ending balance of 3 billion 769 and the first item that increases the account is net income. Taken directly from the income statement that's followed by other comprehensive loss.

This category represents any gains and losses that are not reported on the income statement because they are unrealized. For example, if you have an investment that's decreased in value but you haven't sold it yet that's considered unrealized until you actually sell it in the future. Next we see that Wholefoods has paid dividends this year to their shareholders. Now in a comprehensive accounting class we would analyze each of these line items in detail but for our purposes I really want you to understand the intention of this statement it's showing the investors or the owners exactly what happened to their stake in the business over this period of time.

I also want to mention a few definitions you'll notice there's a line item called Treasury shares this represents shares or stock that a company has repurchased and holds in its own Treasury. This shows up as a negative number that decreases equity. In accounting we call this a contra equity account shares outstanding represent the number of shares currently in the hands of investors. From a company's perspective they'll record an increase in common stock account when they sell shares to the public so their common stock value is the market price at the time the shares were sold directly by the company multiplied by the number of shares sold.

This represents the book value of common stock that's the amount presented on their financial statements. I want to contrast this with the market value which represents the secondary trading that occurs so for example if I own shares and Whole Foods and I subsequently sell them for a higher price this isn't recorded in the company's books. The market value of a company is the number of outstanding shares times the current market price so that's not on the balance sheet. I also want you to just take a step back and think theoretically about stockholders equity remember if you rearrange the balance sheet equation it would become assets minus liabilities equals stockholders equity.

So if a company were to liquidate all of their assets and pay off all of their liabilities whatever's left goes to the owners of the investors. Sometimes you may see a company with a negative stockholders equity value what does that mean? Be wary this can happen for a variety of reasons for example if a company has liabilities that are greater than their assets there's a decline in their currency positions or it could be based on accounting methods that carry forward losses from prior years. These are beyond the scope of our discussion but be wary of negative stockholders equity.

We'll continue our discussion with the remaining financial statements but for now I want you to take some to review the statement of stockholders equity for the company of your choosing.
